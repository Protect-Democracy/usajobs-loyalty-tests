From the descriptions below, select the one response that best describes your highest level of experience and/training performing Rigger Worker duties. Your claim must be supported in your resume.
 I am able to perform the full range of the most difficult and complex Rigger Worker duties with a high degree of independence and initiative. The work that I have performed extensively has included the tasks of a Rigger Worker on a traditionally rigged sailing vessel including 1. Climbing aloft using ratlines or ratbars and stepping out on the footropes to work on the yardarm of yards located up to 100' above the water to paint, tar, remove shackles and hooks, tie knots, and secure gear; 2. Extensive experience of fabricating new or replacement standing and running rigging complete elements from direction by a supervisor including worming, parceling, serving, splicing, and seizing elements; 3. Accurately measuring with a tape measure and calipers in both English and metric systems; 4. Following patterns for canvas work and sewing sails, covers, awnings, bags, mast boots, and other canvas gear by traditional palm, needle, and bench hook using flat and round seams; 5. Seizing blocks and falls into strops to make whip, gun, luff, and double tackle purchases; 6. Laying up Mathew Walker and other stopper knots in 3 and 4 strand lanyards and reeving deadeyes and hearts; 7. Whipping the ends of lines with palm and needle and common whippings; 8. Rigging under direction all blocks, topropes, stoppers, gantlines, and taglines for striking yards and fidded upper masts to deck using a capstan or windlass, and setting up under direction all gear for sending those spars back aloft; 9. Expertly eye-splicing 3 and 4 strand fiber rope, and occasionally splicing 6 strand wire rope using a Liverpool or modified Liverpool splice; 10. Short splicing and long splicing fiber rope; 11. Differentiating between natural fiber, polyester, and polypropylene rope; 12. Selecting proper fids, marlinspikes, heaving mallets, and serving mallets for the job at hand, and using them correctly; 13. Rigging under direction braces, lifts, halliards, clewlines, buntlines, sheets, and tacks on a square sail, and throat and peak halliards, sheets, and jiggers on a gaff rigged sail; 14. Clapping on flat, round, and racking seizings, and knowing when each is to be used; 15. Proficiently tying the following knots: Square (reef) knot, Sheet bend/becket hitch, Double sheet bend, Bowline, Bowline on a bight, Running bowline, Figure eight, Overhand knot, Half hitch, Round turn and 2 half hitches, Buntline hitch, Clove hitch, Constrictor knot, Fisherman's bend/anchor bend, Rolling hitch (around spar), Magnus hitch (around spar), Midshipman's hitch (rigger's hitch) (around rope), Blackwall hitch (on a hook).
 I am able to perform 11 of the 15 tasks listed in "A" above, on my own initiative and under general supervision subject to occasional inspection.
 I am able to perform 8 of the 15 tasks listed in "A" above without more than normal supervision.
 I have no on-the-job training or experience performing the tasks listed in "A" above, but I am willing to learn.
