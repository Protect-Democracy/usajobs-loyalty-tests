7.
You are conducting an industrial capability assessment (study) for the M-22 "Griffin" Tactical Drone, a system critical to Combatant Commander wartime requirements. During your data collection with the prime contractor, they assure you that production is healthy and they possess a 6-month stockpile of drone's high-density thermal sensor module. However, your independent supply chain mapping reveals a critical risk: the Tier 3 sub-vendor that manufacturers the specialty aerospace alloy required for the sensor module is facing imminent financial insolvency and plans to shut down operations in 90 days. Your initial analysis indicates their insolvency is driven by excessive scrap rates (manufacturing waste) and inefficient batch processing. They are currently the sole domestic source for this alloy. Based on this assessment what is your most appropriate programmatic recommendation to mitigate this risk? (Select all that apply)

 A. Propose an immediate “industrial solution” that shares and reduces risks. Deploy a cross-functional team to conduct Value Stream Mapping (VSM) at the Tier-3 facility to identify the root causes of the scrap and invest program funds in targeted access to tooling upgrades and recommend process controls to stabilize their yield, thereby preserving the domestic capability.
 B. Request the prime contractor executes a “supply solution” by buying out all remaining usable alloy inventory from the Tier-3 supplier to create an additional buffer. Concurrently, initiate a DMAIC (Define, Measure, Analyze Improve, Control) project to methodically identify a new domestic supplier over the next 12 months.
 C. Apply “Lean” manufacturing principles by advising the prime contractor that a 6-month stockpile represents excess inventory waste. Recommend they draw down their stockpile to Just-In-Time levels to save warehousing costs, assuming the commercial market with naturally correct the Tier-3 failure.
 D. Issue a formal mandate demanding the failing Tier-3 supplier immediately achieve a Six Sigma quality level (3.4 defects per million opportunities) within 30 days at their own expense, warning them that failure will result in the prime contractor terminating their sub-contract.
