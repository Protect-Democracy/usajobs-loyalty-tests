 The willingness to accept any assignment, regardless of capability.
 The appropriate technical competence, proven analytical skills in tracing system transactions, and the ability to work independently to meet the defined deadline.
 The longest tenure on the team, regardless of their recent performance reviews.

 Have a peer complete all future receipt transactions for the employee.
 Assume the employee will eventually figure it out without intervention.

11.
Task: Works with vendors, contractors, managers, supervisors and staff throughout the medical center and from other agencies on logistics issues.
When resolving a logistics issue involving a vendor, a clinical manager, and the delivery contractor, the Supervisor's primary customer service responsibility is to:

 Decide immediately who is at fault for the problem.
 Side immediately with the clinical manager, regardless of the facts.
 Refuse to talk to the vendor and insist they communicate only through email.
 Maintain a neutral, professional stance, facilitate communication between all parties, and work towards a solution that ensures continuity of supply for the medical center.


12.
Task: Maintains effective interpersonal relationships with peers, subordinates, inter-service staff and others by dealing with operational priorities of the service and other areas affected by Logistics.
When dealing with a conflict between the supply section's priority list and an urgent, conflicting demand from another service, the Supervisor should:

 Communicate the current operational priorities clearly, actively listen to the inter-service staff's specific need, and collaboratively negotiate an adjusted, mutually acceptable solution.
 Tell the other service their priority list is irrelevant and follow their own.
 Only involve the highest-level executive to resolve the minor conflict.
 Stop all other work until the conflict naturally resolves itself.


13.
Task: Maintains close contact with service lines to ensure that critical or urgent requests are processed timely and support both expected and unexpected requirements.
To ensure unexpected critical requirements (e.g., disaster response, sudden epidemic) are supported, the Supervisor must maintain contact with service lines to:

 Get advance notice of all patient diagnoses.
 Establish and periodically test pre-defined communication protocols and priority channels for emergency supply needs and validate the readiness of contingency stock levels.
 Only accept unexpected requests in writing after 5 PM.
 Keep a list of the service line manager's personal phone numbers.


14.
Task: Provides assistance and educates customers in utilizing local logistics policies and procedures.
A clinical customer is confused about the process for returning damaged or compromised stock. The most appropriate education provided by the Supervisor should be:

 Simply handing them a copy of the 50-page policy manual.
 Advising them to discard the damaged stock immediately.
 A concise, step-by-step procedural demonstration of the system transactions and physical handling required, emphasizing policy compliance and the rationale for protecting the supply chain.
 Telling them to ask another staff member next time they have a question.


15.
Task: Makes sound recommendations concerning standard commercial items and other associated terminologies for effective procurement actions.
A clinical service requests a specialty item using ambiguous, non-standard terminology. The Supervisor's sound recommendation for procurement is to:

 Immediately purchase the item from the only vendor that understands the request.
 Tell the customer they cannot have the item until they rewrite their request using technical jargon.
 Research the item's standard commercial terminology and product specification (NSN or equivalent), advising the customer on how to use standard terms to ensure a competitive and compliant procurement action.
 Use the ambiguous term on the purchase order, risking non-compliant acquisition.

16.
Task: Ensures items are distributed according to established or ad hoc requirements using the generic inventory package (GIP).
To ensure items are distributed using GIP according to ad hoc (unexpected) requirements, the Supervisor must utilize the system to:

 Generate a historical usage report from last year.
 Override or adjust standard distribution schedules to create an expedited picking list and route, while documenting the specific ad hoc requirement and authorization within the system.
 Prevent any items from leaving the warehouse until the next scheduled daily run.
 Only allow the most senior employee to input ad hoc requests into the system.


17.
Task: Utilizes a computerized inventory system to ensure sterility and integrity of the supplies and material and the data integrity of the inventory management system.
The most effective system utility for monitoring the sterility and integrity of temperature-sensitive medical supplies is:

 The ability to print paper labels for every item.
 The capability to change the item's location from the main warehouse to the clinic.
 The feature that tracks the number of times the system has been backed up.
 The system's capacity to integrate with temperature monitoring devices and flag items exceeding acceptable thresholds in a central alert dashboard for immediate removal from circulation.


18.
Task: Oversees the computerized inventory management systems and recalls to ensure outdated or otherwise compromised supplies and material are removed from usage area.
During a national product recall, the Supervisor must use the computerized inventory system to immediately:

 Query the system for all affected item numbers, locations, and quantities on hand (including secondary locations), generate a list for rapid removal, and flag the item as restricted/non-issuable.
 Call the vendor and ask them to physically remove the items from the facility.
 Wait for a paper notice to be mailed from the FDA before taking any action.
 Only check the main central warehouse and ignore all secondary storage points.


19.
Task: Develops, catalogs data, levels and replenishment needs for new items, plan requirements, while ensuring both quality and quantity measures are met, and to expedite delivery of urgently needed items.
When developing the GIP catalog data for a new surgical item, the most critical data points the Supervisor must ensure are accurately entered for quality and quantity control are:

 The supplier's website address and the item's color.
 The Unit of Issue (U/I), Issue Multiple, Shelf Life/Expiration Parameters, and the establishment of the initial Par Level and Reorder Point based on projected clinical demand.
 The preferred snack of the surgical team.
 The initials of the person who requested the item.
