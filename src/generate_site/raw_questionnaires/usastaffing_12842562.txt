1.
1. Screen-Out) Do you have the ability to perform the duties of the Electrical Equipment Repairer without more than normal supervision? Related experience includes:  Working knowledge of work orders as well as a sound knowledge of hospital routine, medical terminology, anatomy and physiology, especially those required in the care and treatment of patients in motorized wheelchairs. Working with electrical theory as it pertains to conductance, resistance, capacity in relation to direct current. Practical knowledge and understanding of electronics to identify and replace components with obvious defects including resistors, damaged boards and be able to use testing equipment to trouble shoot electronics.  Setting up and operating computer-controlled automatic test equipment (ATE) and other specialized test equipment to perform both pretest and final checkout of electrical equipment. Making mechanical repairs and adjustments such as replacing springs, bearings, brushes, gears, clutches and other items such as gears, breaks and related parts on a variety of both old and new wheelchairs.  Keeping up-to-date on any certifications of equipment to include but not limited to ever changing electronic technology on newer/updated equipment, especially the highly technical motorized tilt-in-space wheelchairs utilized by spinal cord injured individuals. A specific length of training and experience is not required, but you must show evidence of training or experience of sufficient scope and quality of your ability to do the work of this position which includes experience in repair of mobility equipment such as: wheelchairs, scooters, powerchairs and walkers. Ability to test, troubleshoot and replace motors, electronics, batteries and provide mechanical repairs.   Your experience must be detailed in your resume to receive credit.
