You are the Chief Psychologist at a high-security federal correctional institution. At 2:30 PM on a Friday, you receive an urgent call from the Special Housing Unit where an inmate with known paranoid schizophrenia has barricaded himself in his cell, is shouting that correctional officers are part of a conspiracy to poison him, has flooded his cell, and is threatening to harm himself if anyone attempts to enter. He has fashioned what appears to be a ligature from torn bedding and is holding a makeshift weapon made from a broken piece of his cell fixture. The Special Investigative Services lieutenant reports that the inmate is becoming increasingly agitated and has stopped responding to officers' verbal commands. The Warden has activated the institution's crisis response team and is requesting your immediate involvement. The hostage negotiation team is en route but won't arrive for 45 minutes due to coming from another facility.

Which action represents the most effective and appropriate crisis response approach in this situation?
 Immediately proceed to the Special Housing Unit and position yourself in a safe location where you can communicate with the inmate. Use your crisis communication training to establish verbal contact, speaking in a calm, non-threatening tone to begin de-escalating his paranoid ideation and agitation. Implement confrontation avoidance techniques by acknowledging his distress without validating the delusional content, while using active listening to build rapport. Assess his immediate suicide risk through verbal interaction and observation. Coordinate closely with the crisis response team and correctional staff, providing real-time clinical guidance on management strategies for this specific inmate's psychiatric condition. Advise staff on medications the inmate may be prescribed that could affect his behavior and cognition. Continue engagement until the hostage negotiation team arrives, providing them with a comprehensive briefing on the inmate's mental status, triggers observed, and approaches that were effective. Follow up by leading a post-incident debriefing with staff to review the crisis response and provide education on recognizing and managing future psychiatric emergencies.
 Report to the Special Housing Unit to assess the situation and consult with correctional supervisors about the inmate's psychiatric history and current medications. Attempt to communicate with the inmate to calm him down, advising staff to avoid actions that might escalate his paranoid symptoms. Provide recommendations to the crisis response team about managing a patient with severe mental illness, including suggesting they wait for him to become less agitated before attempting cell entry. Document the incident and conduct a follow-up training session with staff on crisis intervention techniques for mentally ill inmates.
 Respond to the Special Housing Unit and consult with the ranking correctional officer about the inmate's behavior patterns and previous mental health crises. Provide guidance to staff about avoiding confrontational approaches that could trigger increased agitation in someone experiencing psychotic symptoms. Review the inmate's mental health record to determine his baseline functioning and current medications. Recommend that staff maintain verbal contact while waiting for the specialized negotiation team, and offer to be available if clinical consultation is needed during the negotiation process.
 Review the inmate's psychological file to understand his diagnosis and treatment history, contact your clinical supervisor or the Regional Psychologist to discuss appropriate crisis intervention strategies for an inmate with paranoid schizophrenia who is barricaded and threatening self-harm, travel to the scene to observe the hostage negotiation team's approach when they arrive, and participate in the post-crisis case review meeting to discuss what was learned from this emergency situation.
