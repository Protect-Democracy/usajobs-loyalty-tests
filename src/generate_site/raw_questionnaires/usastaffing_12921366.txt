 The discrepancy requires a formal "Loss/Gain" report to be filed with DLA Energy. This report documents the difference between the paper and wet inventories, which is then investigated to determine the cause, such as accounting errors, meter inaccuracies, or an allowable in-transit loss.

11.
Scenario: A Combatant Commander's operational plan requires the establishment of a Joint Logistics Over-the-Shore (JLOTS) operation to support forces in an undeveloped theater. The plan calls for the delivery of F-76 marine diesel for naval assets and JP-8 for aviation and ground forces. However, due to limited sealift, only a single Offshore Petroleum Discharge System (OPDS) can be deployed in the initial phase.
What is the most significant logistical complexity the Joint Petroleum Officer (JPO) must solve for in this scenario?

 The central problem is managing "product sequencing and interface." The JPO must synchronize the arrival of tankers carrying different products with the CCMD's operational priorities. This involves deciding which fuel to pump first, scheduling sufficient time for line flushing and "pigging" to prevent cross-contamination, and having the correct segregated storage ready on the beach. A failure to correctly sequence these events could result in the right total quantity of fuel ashore, but the wrong type available for critical assets.
 The JPO's primary problem is securing enough 5,000-gallon collapsible drums to store the fuel once it gets to the beach, as this is the only way to manage fuel ashore.
 The critical complexity is "product integrity." Using a single pipeline from the OPDS to the beach creates a high risk of co-mingling F-76 and JP-8, which have different specifications. The JPO must create a plan to flush the lines between product transfers, which consumes time and generates fuel-water waste.
 The main challenge is the slow flow rate of the OPDS system, which will create a bottleneck and delay the offload of the multiple tankers required to meet the initial demand.
