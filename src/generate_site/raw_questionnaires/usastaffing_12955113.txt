3.
How do you ensure that OIG audits, investigations, and evaluations comply with the professional standards (e.g., DoD IG Investigations Manual, GAO Yellow Book), and how do those standards interact with administrative law principles?

 By implementing a robust quality assurance system that includes mandatory training, supervisory review at each stage, and a final legal sufficiency review. This interaction is critical because following these standards ensures our findings are based on sufficient, competent, and relevant evidence, which directly supports the legal requirement that our official actions are not "arbitrary and capricious" and can withstand judicial or administrative scrutiny.
 By ensuring all auditors and investigators receive annual training on the most recent updates to the Yellow Book and Blue Book. These standards are primarily for maintaining professional credibility and are generally treated as separate from the administrative law principles that govern the agency's program activities.
 By submitting our office to an external peer review every three years as mandated. Passing the peer review is the definitive measure of compliance. The standards themselves are internal to the oversight community and do not have a direct connection to administrative law, which is handled by lawyers.
 By having OIG legal counsel review all draft reports to ensure they contain the correct legal citations and disclaimers. The professional standards guide the fieldwork, but adherence to administrative law is achieved by the legal team's final review and edits before publication.

As the Inspector General, what is the MOST critical technical issue you should identify?

 The report's formatting is inconsistent with OIG style guidelines, which may confuse the reader.
 The team failed to meet the Generally Accepted Government Auditing Standards (GAGAS) for evidence, specifically regarding the need for sufficient, appropriate evidence to support the findings.
 The team did not include a formal recommendation for disciplinary action against the component's leadership, which is a required element of all audit reports.
 The auditors should have used more advanced data visualization software to present their findings, even if the underlying data was weak.


8.
An investigation into contract fraud involves allegations that a senior official is communicating with a vendor via an encrypted messaging application on a personal device. Your lead investigator suggests issuing an administrative subpoena to the tech company that owns the app to obtain the content of the messages. What is your MOST appropriate response as the Inspector General?

 Approve the subpoena, as the Inspector General Act provides broad authority to access all records necessary to conduct an investigation.
 Approve a modified subpoena, but only for the "metadata" (e.g., who messaged whom and when), as this is all an administrative subpoena can legally obtain from a provider in this context.
 Table the decision and direct the team to first interview the senior official, as their voluntary consent would be the easiest way to obtain the messages.
 Deny the request and direct the team to partner with the DoD IG. Your technical competence should inform you that obtaining the content of private, encrypted communications from a provider typically requires a criminal search warrant, which is outside the DWIA OIG's administrative authority.


9.
As the Inspector General, outline your comprehensive investigative plan for gathering the necessary information. Describe the sequence of your major steps, the legal authorities and tools you would employ to obtain evidence (particularly the digital communications), and the strategies you would use to address the challenges posed by a fearful informant, a reluctant witness, and a potentially uncooperative subject.

 My first step is to immediately issue an OIG administrative subpoena directly to the Director for their personal cell phone and demand access to their personal email accounts. The Inspector General Act gives us the right to access all records, and this is the most direct path to the key evidence. We will then bring in the reluctant grant panel member for a mandatory interview. Once we have the phone and the panel member's statement, we will interview the Director and use the threat of a criminal referral to ensure cooperation.
 First, my team will discreetly corroborate the CI's core claims using internal agency records and public data (e.g., grant award data, non-profit registration). Concurrently, we will analyze the grant review panel's scoring data to identify anomalies. Once we establish a solid predicate, we will partner with the Department of Justice to seek a criminal search warrant for the personal email and encrypted messages, as this is beyond our administrative subpoena authority. Only after securing this digital evidence would we strategically interview the reluctant panel member, using the evidence to encourage cooperation. The subject director would be interviewed last, with counsel present, fully prepared to confront them with the evidence gathered.
 I will prioritize the human sources. First, I will have my investigator approach the reluctant grant review panel member for a voluntary interview, offering confidentiality to build trust. Based on their statement, we will then bring in the Director for a formal interview under a Garrity warning. If the Director denies the allegations, we will use the panel member's statement and the CI's information as leverage to pressure them to consent to a search of their personal devices.
 My office will immediately open a formal investigation and send a notification of investigation to the Director to prevent any destruction of evidence. We will then coordinate administrative requests to the telecommunications provider (e.g., Verizon, AT&T) and the tech company (e.g., Apple, Signal) for the content of the personal emails and encrypted messages. While waiting for that data, we will interview the CI to get a sworn statement and then interview the other panel members.
