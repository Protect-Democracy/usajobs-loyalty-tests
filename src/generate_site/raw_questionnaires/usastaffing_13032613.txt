Are you a current or former federal employee displaced from the Department of Justice?

The Career Transition Assistance Plan (CTAP) is an intra-agency program that helps surplus or displaced federal employees improve their chances of finding a new job in their agency, by giving them selection priority over other applicants, as long as they're qualified for the job.

To be eligible under CTAP you must meet all of the following:
You are located in the same local commuting area of this vacancy; AND
You are applying to a position that is at (or below) the grade level of the position from which you were (or will) be separated from; AND
You are applying to a position that does not have greater promotion potential than the position from which you were (or will be) separated from; AND
Your last performance rating of record is at least fully successful, or the equivalent.
To verify your eligibility: You must submit appropriate supporting documentation, such as a Reduction in Force (RIF) separation notice or a Proposed Removal/Separation notice to include your latest performance rating (if the notice does not have this information included, you must submit a copy of your most recent performance appraisal) and a copy of your latest Notification of Personnel Action (SF-50). Assessment 2
This assessment measures the technical competencies required for the Unit Manager position. For each question, select the answer that best represents the most effective and appropriate approach. Only one answer per question will be scored.


1.
You are the Unit Manager overseeing a housing unit when you receive urgent information that one of your inmates, Johnson, has just lost his mother in a fatal car accident. Johnson has a history of violent outbursts and self-harm attempts. He is currently in the day room with other inmates and is unaware of the situation. You need to inform him of this devastating news while managing the immediate safety concerns and emotional crisis that will likely follow. The institutional psychologist is not immediately available, but a Case Manager is present in the unit. Other inmates are watching, and the atmosphere is tense because Johnson is a respected figure among the inmate population.

Which action represents the most effective and appropriate oral communication approach in this situation?

 Immediately pull Johnson aside to a private office, calmly inform him of his mother's death using clear and compassionate language, remain present to gauge his emotional response, coordinate with the Case Manager to ensure continuous supervision, and communicate with security staff about potential crisis intervention needs while offering information about available counseling services.
 Prepare a comprehensive presentation about grief counseling resources and institutional bereavement procedures, schedule a meeting with Johnson for later in the day when the psychologist arrives, and conduct a formal briefing with all unit staff about the situation to ensure everyone is informed of the proper protocols for delivering such news.
 Call Johnson to your office and provide him with written documentation about his mother's death, direct him to read the materials, and instruct him to meet with his Case Manager during the next scheduled appointment to discuss funeral arrangements and any available options for attending services.
 Organize a staff meeting to develop a coordinated communication plan, draft a memorandum outlining the notification procedures, assign the Case Manager to inform Johnson during the afternoon unit activities, and prepare follow-up documentation for the inmate's file regarding the notification process.
 None of the above.

Martinez has filed multiple administrative remedies claiming he is being denied programming opportunities due to his gang status. The Regional Director has personally requested a comprehensive report on this case. Additionally, a Congressional office has sent an inquiry based on a complaint from Martinez's attorney, citing potential civil rights violations and referencing complex case law including Turner v. Safley and Sandin v. Conner. Your Warden needs a detailed written response that addresses the legal precedents, documents all programming decisions, justifies the security restrictions, and explains the institution's interpretation of relevant Bureau policies regarding gang member management. This response will be reviewed by the Office of General Counsel.

Which action represents the most effective and appropriate written communication approach in this situation?

 Write a detailed transfer referral and comprehensive response memorandum to the Regional Director and Congressional inquiry that analyzes the complex constitutional case law, documents all security-based programming decisions with specific policy citations, explains the gang designation process and its legal foundation, addresses each allegation with supporting evidence, and provides a thorough justification of the institution's actions in accordance with Bureau policy and legal precedent.
 Prepare routine correspondence to the Warden summarizing the inmate's current program assignments, draft a standard response to the Congressional inquiry indicating that all decisions were made in accordance with policy, and create a case file memo documenting the inmate's gang affiliation and general security concerns for future reference.
 Assist your supervisor in drafting the response by gathering relevant documents about the inmate's programming history, write a brief memorandum to Martinez advising him of the policies that govern gang member programming restrictions, and help compile the case file materials for review by higher-level staff who will prepare the final response.
 Develop a comprehensive training presentation for unit staff on proper procedures for handling Congressional inquiries, create a lesson plan on gang management policies, and prepare a general memorandum to all inmates in the unit explaining the institution's programming eligibility criteria and security designation procedures.

3.
You are the Unit Manager for a specialized Residential Drug Abuse Program (RDAP) unit. Inmate Peterson is approaching his projected release date and is scheduled to complete the RDAP program in three weeks. However, Peterson recently received a new sentence computation from the Designation and Sentence Computation Center that may affect his release eligibility. His case involves multiple complex factors: he has successfully completed all RDAP requirements, but there are questions about whether his current offense qualifies under the Violent Crime Control and Law Enforcement Act provisions, which could require community notification upon release. Additionally, his early release eligibility under 18 U.S.C. ? 3621(e) is in question due to the nature of his conviction. His Case Manager has recommended early release, but the institution's legal department has flagged potential issues with his sentence structure. You must make a determination about his RDAP early release eligibility and whether notification to state law enforcement is required, interpreting multiple overlapping policies, federal statutes, and Bureau regulations.

Which action represents the most effective and appropriate policy interpretation and application approach in this situation?

 Conduct a comprehensive analysis of Peterson's complete criminal history and current offense, thoroughly review and interpret 18 U.S.C. ? 3621(e) requirements for RDAP early release eligibility, determine whether the Violent Crime Control and Law Enforcement Act notification requirements apply to his specific circumstances, consult the relevant Program Statements on RDAP early release and community notification, make a formal determination on his release eligibility with detailed legal justification, and coordinate with state/local law enforcement regarding notification requirements if applicable.
 Review Peterson's RDAP participation records and institutional adjustment, perform routine case management classification to confirm he has completed all program requirements, interpret the standard eligibility criteria in the RDAP Program Statement, make a recommendation to the Warden regarding his programming completion, and prepare the standard release preparation documentation for general population inmates approaching their release date.
 Serve as a member of the RDAP program review team to evaluate Peterson's case, review the court documents from his sentencing to verify his commitment offense, interpret and apply the agency's RDAP policy in processing his program completion paperwork, conduct the intake processing for his transition to community supervision, and complete the routine investigation of his institutional conduct record.
 Coordinate with the Case Manager to gather all relevant policy materials, assist in researching the legal questions about his sentence computation, review the institution's standard operating procedures for RDAP releases, help prepare documentation for the Regional Counsel's review, and compile Peterson's file for the Warden's final decision on the complex legal matters.

4.
Your institution is experiencing significant challenges with inmates transitioning back to the community. The current Release Orientation Program has a standard curriculum that covers basic topics, but recidivism data shows that inmates from your institution are struggling with specific reentry issues: 40% are returning due to employment failures, 30% due to housing instability, and 25% due to lack of family/community support connections. The Warden has tasked you with addressing this problem. You have identified gaps in the existing program structure and have researched evidence-based practices from other institutions and the National Institute of Corrections. You have the authority and resources to make substantial changes to how release preparation is delivered in your units. The Executive Staff will review your proposal next month and is expecting a comprehensive solution that will measurably improve outcomes.

Which action represents the most effective and appropriate correctional programs administration approach in this situation?

 Develop and implement a completely redesigned Institution Release Preparation Program that is specifically tailored to address the identified reentry challenges, including new curriculum modules on employment readiness and job retention strategies, partnerships with community housing organizations, enhanced family reunification components, measurable outcome indicators, staff training on the new approach, and a structured evaluation process to assess the program's effectiveness with recommendations to Executive Staff for institution-wide implementation.
 After reviewing each inmate's assessment results and reentry needs, make individualized program recommendations by changing inmates' program assignments to better match their specific risks (such as moving some inmates from basic education to employment skills programs), provide detailed input to Executive Staff about the institution's overall Release Preparation operations and suggest some modifications to improve outcomes, and monitor the existing recreation and education programs to ensure they are addressing employment readiness.
 Coordinate the existing volunteer and contract programs to ensure community organizations are available to assist with release preparation, help administer the current urinalysis program to ensure inmates are remaining drug-free prior to release, assist the education department in creating new lesson plans focused on employment topics, and help prepare a presentation on the institution's current Correctional Programs for the next Community Relations Board meeting to build community partnerships.
 Research best practices in release preparation programming from other institutions, compile data on the current program's outcomes, draft a detailed memorandum to the Warden outlining the problems with the existing program and suggesting that a working group be formed to study the issue, and volunteer to serve on the committee that will eventually make recommendations for program improvements to Executive Staff.
 None of the above.
