Current Department of Army Civilian Employees Applying on Time-Limited Assignments

Assessment 2
INSTRUCTIONS: You will now complete a Situational Judgement Test (SJT). This assessment presents realistic job-related scenarios. For each one, select the response that best reflects how you would handle the situation. Each question has four response options. These range from the most appropriate to the least appropriate action, but the options are presented in random order. Your task is to choose the option you believe is the best course of action in a federal workplace setting.

-There are no right or wrong answers.
-Your responses should reflect sound judgment and professional decision-making.
-This test evaluates how you approach workplace situations, not your prior experience or background. Please answer honestly and thoughtfully. Your responses will help determine your qualifications for this position.


1.
You are overseeing a multi-year real estate acquisition program that has fallen behind schedule due to unexpected environmental compliance issues. Your team is requesting additional resources, but your supervisor has indicated budget constraints may limit options. Three critical acquisitions are needed to maintain the overall program timeline, and stakeholders are expressing concern about delays. How do you proceed?

 Focus team efforts on the most straightforward acquisition to demonstrate progress, while simultaneously working with environmental compliance staff to develop solutions for the more complex properties that can be implemented as resources become available.
 Conduct a comprehensive program analysis to identify which acquisitions are most critical to mission success, reallocate existing resources from lower-priority activities, and develop a phased approach that maintains essential milestones while addressing environmental compliance requirements through established Corps procedures.
 Request emergency funding authorization from higher headquarters, emphasizing the critical nature of the program delays, while directing staff to expedite all acquisition activities regardless of the additional environmental compliance requirements.
 Postpone the environmental compliance activities until after the acquisitions are completed, since the program timeline takes precedence over regulatory requirements that can be addressed in post-acquisition phases.
 Meet with your supervisor to present a detailed cost-benefit analysis of the resource needs, propose alternative funding sources or timeline adjustments, and work with environmental specialists to streamline compliance processes while maintaining regulatory adherence.

Federal partners are requesting status updates, and local communities are expressing concerns about property impacts. What is your approach?

 Request federal intervention to override the new state requirements, arguing that the infrastructure project's national importance should take precedence over local regulatory changes that were not part of the original program planning.
 Proceed with acquisitions using the original appraisal methods in both states, since federal programs should not be subject to changing state requirements that were implemented after program initiation.
 Establish a comprehensive coordination framework that includes regular stakeholder meetings, develop state-specific acquisition strategies that comply with all requirements, create a master schedule that accounts for regulatory differences, and implement proactive communication protocols with all parties.
 Work directly with state officials to understand the new requirements and negotiate reasonable implementation timelines, while developing contingency plans for the affected acquisitions and maintaining transparent communication with federal partners about realistic delivery schedules.
 Focus on accelerating acquisitions in the state without new requirements while working with legal counsel to determine compliance options for the state with new appraisal requirements, keeping stakeholders informed of the bifurcated approach.

Property owners have been notified of the original compensation amounts, and acquisition negotiations are scheduled to begin next week. How do you handle this situation?

 Apply the new guidance to projects that haven't completed appraisals while consulting with legal counsel about the project with completed appraisals, ensuring all stakeholders understand the regulatory requirements and developing a timeline for implementing necessary changes.
 Implement the new guidance on future appraisals while seeking clarification from headquarters about whether the guidance applies retroactively to completed appraisals, maintaining transparency with property owners about potential methodology changes.
 Request an exemption from headquarters for your ongoing projects since changing methodologies mid-process would create administrative burden and potential legal exposure that outweighs the benefits of implementing the new guidance.
 Immediately halt negotiations on all affected projects, conduct a thorough analysis of how the new guidance impacts each acquisition, re-evaluate completed appraisals where necessary, and develop a communication strategy to inform property owners of any changes while ensuring full compliance with the updated methodology.
 Continue with negotiations using the original appraisals since property owners have already been notified, while applying the new guidance only to future projects to avoid confusion and potential legal complications with current negotiations.

 Expedite approval of the report since the deficiencies are technical rather than substantive, while noting the issues for future training purposes and ensuring that similar problems are avoided in subsequent reports.
 Meet with the report author to discuss the deficiencies and provide mentoring on the updated guidelines, establish a revised timeline that allows for proper corrections, and communicate with the project manager about the importance of regulatory compliance for long-term project success.
 Return the report to staff with specific guidance on the deficiencies and required corrections, provide technical assistance to ensure proper application of the updated guidelines, and work with the project manager to adjust timelines as necessary to maintain quality and regulatory compliance.
 Approve the report with conditions that require the identified issues to be addressed during the negotiation phase, while providing detailed guidance to staff about the proper application of the guidelines for future reports.
 Approve the report as submitted since the timeline pressures outweigh the technical deficiencies, and the negotiation process will likely resolve any issues that arise from the analytical gaps.

The overall program timeline cannot be extended. How do you manage this situation?

 Request additional resources and timeline extensions from program leadership, emphasizing the unforeseen nature of the complications, while directing staff to expedite all acquisition activities to make up time wherever possible.
 Immediately convene a cross-functional team to assess all complications, develop property-specific resolution strategies, create a revised execution plan that prioritizes acquisitions with fewer complications, and establish parallel workstreams to address title, environmental, and historic preservation issues while maintaining program objectives.
 Categorize properties by complexity level and focus initial efforts on straightforward acquisitions to maintain momentum, while simultaneously engaging specialists to develop solutions for complicated properties and working with stakeholders to address community concerns through established consultation processes.
