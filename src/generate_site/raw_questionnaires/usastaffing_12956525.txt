Assessment 2
1.
You are leading a critical server migration project with a tight deadline. Two of your most experienced senior administrators have a strong disagreement on the best technical approach for data replication, causing a stalemate and putting the project schedule at risk. What is the best immediate action to take as the supervisor?
 Assign a junior administrator to research both approaches and present a recommendation to break the tie.
 Allow the debate to continue for another day to see if the administrators can resolve it on their own without intervention.
 Facilitate a meeting with both administrators to have them present the pros and cons of their respective approaches, mediate the discussion to find a consensus or make a final, evidence-based decision to move the project forward.
 Implement the less risky of the two options to avoid potential project delays, even if it might not be the most optimal solution.


2.
Your team is consistently missing deadlines for routine system patching, citing a heavy workload from unplanned outages. Team morale is low. Which of the following actions would be most effective in addressing the root cause of the issue? (Select all that apply.)
 A. Request additional headcount based on the team's feedback about being overworked.
 B. Implement a formal review of the team's workload, ticket prioritization process, and time allocation to identify bottlenecks, and then work with the team to rebalance duties and create a protected time block for maintenance activities.
 C. Invest in a new, automated patching tool to reduce the manual effort required for maintenance tasks.
 D. Implement a dedicated on-call rotation to handle unplanned outages, thereby freeing up resources for routine patching, cross-training team members on critical systems to distribute knowledge.
 E. All of the above.


3.
An administrator on your team, who is typically a solid performer, has made the same critical configuration error twice in the last month, causing brief service interruptions. How should you address this performance issue?
 Assign a senior peer to shadow the administrator's work for the next month to prevent further errors.
 Note the pattern and begin compiling documentation for a potential formal disciplinary action if it happens a third time.
 Meet with the administrator privately to discuss the specific errors, ask questions to understand the cause, review the correct procedure, offer additional support, and establish a plan for additional training or peer review to ensure it doesn't happen again.
 Update the team's standard operating procedures (SOPs) to add more checks and balances for the specific task that was performed incorrectly.


4.
As the supervisor, you are responsible for developing the annual budget for your systems administration team. Which of the following is the most critical component to include in your budget justification for new server hardware?

 A detailed project plan for the migration, including a full list of personnel and timelines.
 A technical analysis from the hardware vendor detailing the superior performance of the new models.
 A business case justifying the investment by demonstrating its alignment with mission-critical objectives, detailing projected capacity demands, analyzing the total cost of ownership (TCO), and outlining the operational hazards associated with neglecting the upgrade.
 A summary of recent system failures and outages attributed to the aging hardware.

 D. A process for de-provisioning accounts for departing employees.

 Documenting your team's current internal processes for patching of servers

Users are reporting intermittent and slow access to a critical web application hosted on a server cluster your team manages. Initial checks show that server CPU and memory usage are normal. What is the most logical next step in troubleshooting the problem?
 Temporarily add more servers to the cluster to see if the increased capacity resolves the issue.
 Analyze network traffic logs and load balancer performance metrics to identify potential bottlenecks or misconfigurations between the users and the server cluster.
 Check the application's error logs on each server for any new or unusual entries.
 Fail over to the disaster recovery site to determine if the issue is site-specific.

 A. Replace the failed RAID controller with a new, compatible unit.
 B. Notify business stakeholders of the estimated time to recovery based on the SLA.
 C. Restore the RAID configuration from the saved configuration file or rebuild it manually according to documentation.
 D. Perform a full data restore from the most recent known-good backup after the array is rebuilt.
 E. All of the above.

After a recent OS patch, a legacy application that cannot be easily updated has stopped working. The application vendor states they do not support the new OS version. Reverting the patch is not an option due to security policy. Which of the following is the MOST viable long-term solution?
 Document the issue as an accepted operational risk and inform users that the application may have stability problems.
 Procure extended support from the application vendor to develop a custom patch for the new OS version.
 Keep the server in a separate, un-patched OU (Organizational Unit) in Active Directory and disable automatic updates.
 Isolate the application on a dedicated virtual machine (VM) with limited network access and additional monitoring, running the older, compatible OS version until a replacement application can be procured.
