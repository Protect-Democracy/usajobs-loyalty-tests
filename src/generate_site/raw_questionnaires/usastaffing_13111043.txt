The Advanced Mining and Mineral Production Technologies Office consists of three programs: Resource Characterization Technologies Program Mining and Extraction Technologies Program Mineral and Extractive Metallurgical Processing Technologies Program Which of these programs best align with your professional experience, expertise, and interests? Please rank the programs you are both qualified for and interested in working with, in order of preference, with 1 representing your highest level of interest . Only include programs that align with your experience and that you would be interested in supporting. You do not need to rank all three programs; omit any program for which you lack relevant experience or have no interest in working. 1. Resource Characterization Technologies Program The Resource Characterization Program supports research, development, and demonstrations of advanced technologies and analytical approaches to identify, characterize, evaluate, and de-risk domestic mineral resources and potential development sites. The program advances innovative geoscience, prospecting, sensing, analytical, and data-driven resource assessment technologies, along with economic and social science research, to improve resource estimation, recoverability assessments, economic valuation, and project site selection. Activities address diverse domestic resources and feedstocks, including ores, mine tailings and waste, coal and coal-based materials, energy-related waste streams, and industrial process streams. The program also advances tools and methodologies to evaluate environmental, economic, social, and other project development factors to reduce uncertainty, inform siting and investment decisions, facilitate more efficient permitting, and accelerate domestic mineral and metal production. 2. Mining and Extraction Technologies Program The Mining and Extraction Program supports research, development, and demonstrations of advanced and next-generation technologies to improve the efficiency, productivity, safety, and economic viability of domestic mineral extraction and production. The program advances conventional mining technologies, innovative mining systems, automation, sensing, mine safety technologies, operational optimization, and emerging extraction approaches, including in situ mining and mineral production by well. The program also facilitates technology transfer and adaptation of commercially proven technologies from other sectors into existing mining operations to accelerate modernization and deployment. Activities focus on developing, adapting, testing, scaling, and demonstrating integrated mining and extraction technologies across diverse domestic mineral resources to reduce operating costs, improve resource recovery, overcome deployment barriers, and accelerate commercial adoption. 3. Mineral and Extractive Metallurgical Processing Technologies Program The Mineral and Extractive Metallurgical Processing Program supports research, development, and demonstrations of advanced technologies to improve the recovery, separation, refining, and production of minerals and metals from domestic resources. The program advances conventional and next-generation comminution, beneficiation, physical separation, hydrometallurgical, pyrometallurgical, electrochemical, and integrated processing technologies, including impurity removal, process intensification, waste management, and process simulation and validation. The program also facilitates technology transfer and adaptation of commercially proven technologies from other sectors, improves the efficiency and economics of incumbent processing technologies, and reduces supply chain vulnerabilities associated with existing technologies and operations. Activities address diverse domestic feedstocks, including ores, mine tailings and waste, coal and coal-based materials, energy-related waste streams, and industrial process streams, to improve recovery, selectivity, product purity, throughput, resource efficiency, and commercial scalability while reducing processing costs, overcoming deployment barriers, and accelerating domestic mineral and metal production.
