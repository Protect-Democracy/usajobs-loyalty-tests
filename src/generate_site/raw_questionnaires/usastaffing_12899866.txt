15.
The Criminal Investigator position requires moderate to arduous physical exertion involving walking, and running, use of firearms, and exposure to inclement weather. Manual dexterity with comparatively free motion of fingers, wrists, elbows, shoulders, and knee joints is required; arms/hands/legs/feet must function separately. The work requires physical strength and stamina to perform such activities as conducting long periods of surveillance, pursuing and restraining suspects, and carrying heavy equipment that is utilized in investigative efforts.

Appointment will be contingent upon a candidate passing a pre-employment medical examination and may be contingent on a psychological examination to ascertain possession of the physical, emotional, and mental requirements for the position. Any chronic disease or condition affecting the respiratory, cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, musculoskeletal, digestive, nervous, endocrine or genitourinary systems that would impair full performance of the job duties will be evaluated on a case-by-case basis and may be disqualifying. Common reasons for failing the examination involve vision and hearing impairments. Applicants must have distance-corrected visual acuity of 20/20 or better binocularly (using both eyes at once), with at least 20/40 in each eye. Applicants who wear soft contact lenses have no uncorrected distant visual acuity recommendation. For those who wear glasses or hard contacts, uncorrected distant visual acuity should be 20/100 or better binocularly. Applicants require 20/40 corrected visual acuity in the better eye. Moderate or severe color vision deficiency is disqualifying. Hearing in both ears is required. The use of hearing aids are acceptable if they allow localizing of sound. Any medical condition affecting the ability to safely and efficiently perform essential job functions and physical requirements, with the potential for sudden or subtle incapacitation, or otherwise risking the safety of self or others, national security, or the continuity of business operations may be disqualifying.

Instructions: Law enforcement officers and agents are held to a high standard of honesty, integrity and credibility due to the need of public trust and confidence related to their job responsibilities. One of these responsibilities is the requirement to testify at criminal trials. Under GIGLIO v. United States, 405 U.S. 150 (1972), investigative agencies must turn over to prosecutors any potential impeachment evidence with respect to agents/officers involved in court cases. In testifying, an agent/officer's credibility, character of truthfulness and reputation could be used to cast substantial doubt on the evidence and facts of these criminal cases.
