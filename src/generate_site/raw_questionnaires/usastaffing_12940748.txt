Current Department of Army Civilian Employees Applying on Time-Limited Assignments
    
This job opportunity announcement is open to current, permanent Army employees that would occupy the position under a time-limited promotion, reassignment or change to lower grade not to exceed (NTE). You are eligible in this category if you fall into one of the following:

•  ARMY CIVILIAN EMPLOYEES SERVING ON A PERMANENT APPOINTMENT. You must submit a copy of your most recent SF-50, Notification of Personnel Action. Block 24 of your SF-50 must be a "1" or "2" AND block 34 must be a"1." Army employees can access their SF-50 at: MyBiz.

To obtain a copy of SF-50s from employment with the Department of Defense, go to My Biz at https://compo.dcpds.cpms.osd.mil/.

Assessment 2
You will now complete a Situational Judgement Test (SJT). This assessment presents realistic job-related scenarios. For each one, select the response that best reflects how you would handle the situation. Each question has four response options. These range from the most appropriate to the least appropriate action, but the options are presented in random order. Your task is to choose the option you believe is the best course of action in a federal workplace setting. There are no right or wrong answers. Your responses should reflect sound judgment and professional decision-making. This test evaluates how you approach workplace situations, not your prior experience or background. Please answer honestly and thoughtfully. Your responses will help determine your qualifications for this position.


1.
You are designing the electrical distribution system for a new military airfield that includes both runway lighting and hangar facilities. During your analysis, you discover that the proposed 15kV transmission line routing conflicts with the precision approach path indicator (PAPI) system placement, potentially causing electromagnetic interference with navigation equipment. The project deadline is approaching, and the construction contractor is requesting immediate design approval. What is your best course of action?

 Review existing airfield standards for similar installations, calculate minimum separation distances based on standard practices, and adjust the transmission line routing to meet typical clearance requirements without detailed interference analysis.
 Conduct electromagnetic compatibility analysis, coordinate with airfield operations specialists to evaluate alternative transmission routing options, and revise the design to eliminate interference while maintaining system reliability and code compliance.
 Proceed with the original design as planned since the transmission voltage is within normal parameters, and request that navigation equipment be relocated during construction if interference occurs.
 Approve the current design with a recommendation to install additional shielding around the PAPI system during construction, assuming standard separation distances will be sufficient to prevent operational issues.
 Perform load flow analysis to determine if underground cable routing is feasible, consult with the navigation systems engineer, and propose design modifications that separate the transmission and navigation systems by adequate distances.

 Approve both designs as submitted since each team has technical expertise in their area, and instruct the construction contractor to implement whichever approach is more cost-effective during installation.
 Request both teams to provide detailed technical justifications for their approaches, compare their solutions against industry standards, and select the approach that best meets the primary operational requirements of the facility.
