Assessment 2
1.
A critical server system has experienced a catastrophic failure. The team is under pressure to restore service, but two senior IT specialists are in sharp disagreement about the best path forward, causing a stalemate. As the technical lead, which of the following is the BEST initial action to take?
 Ask the junior member of the team to choose one of the two proposed solutions to serve as a tie-breaker.
 Bring both specialists together, acknowledge their expertise, and facilitate a brief, focused discussion on the technical merits of each approach to reach a consensus on the immediate next step.
 Allow each specialist to work on their proposed solution independently and see which one resolves the issue first.
 Make an executive decision to implement one specialist's solution based on their seniority and past successes.


2.
Your team needs to adopt a new server automation technology that requires a significant shift in skills. One of your most experienced team members is resistant to the change, expressing a preference for the current, manual processes they have mastered. As the team lead, what is the MOST effective approach?
 Implement the new technology without the resistant employee's input and assign them to maintain legacy systems exclusively.
 Postpone the technology adoption until all team members, including the resistant one, are fully on board.
 Tell the team member that adopting the new technology is mandatory and not open for discussion.
 Meet with the team member to understand their concerns, emphasizing the benefits of the new technology for both the organization and their professional growth, and involve them in the implementation planning.


3.
A junior system administrator on your team makes a significant configuration error that results in a minor service outage. The team member is visibly distressed about the mistake. What is the MOST appropriate leadership response?
 Discuss the mistake during the next team meeting to ensure everyone learns from the error.
 Immediately create a new, restrictive change management policy to prevent any future configuration errors.
 Focus on restoring the service, then meet privately with the team member to review the incident as a learning opportunity and reinforce the importance of following established change control procedures.
 Take over the team member's responsibilities for the rest of the week to allow them time to recover from the stress of the incident.


4.
Users are intermittently reporting that they cannot access a critical enterprise application. Initial diagnostics show no server outages or network-wide connectivity issues. Which of the following is the MOST logical next step in troubleshooting the problem?
 Ask a sample of users for their workstation's IP address to check for a correlation.
 Review application, server, and firewall logs from the times of the reported incidents to identify any error patterns, anomalous traffic, or failed authentication attempts.
 Advise affected users to clear their browser cache and restart their computers.
 Check the network switch connecting the application server for hardware faults.


5.
A newly deployed patch designed to fix a minor security flaw has caused a critical business application to fail for all users. Which of the following actions should be taken FIRST?
 Immediately initiate the patch rollback procedure to a last-known stable state to restore service.
 Begin developing a hotfix to correct the issue caused by the vendor's patch.
 Escalate the issue to the application vendor's emergency support and wait for their guidance.
 Perform a full diagnostic scan of the server to ensure the patch did not cause other, hidden issues.


6.
Monitoring alerts indicate that the CPU utilization on a primary database server consistently spikes to 100% during a specific one-hour window every afternoon, causing significant performance degradation. What is the MOST effective way to diagnose the root cause?
 Increase the server's CPU priority for the database process.
 Migrate the database to a more powerful server to see if the problem persists.
 Investigate how long the spike has been occurring, correlate the high-utilization window with any scheduled tasks, application jobs, or user reporting activities to identify which specific processes or queries are causing the spike.
 Implement load balancing to distribute the database requests across multiple servers.
