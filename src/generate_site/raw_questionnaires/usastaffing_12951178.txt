Assessment 1
This assessment presents 12 scenario-based situational judgment questions designed to evaluate candidates' competency across four major duty areas for the GS-14 Administrative Investigations Specialist position. Each scenario reflects realistic situations encountered when investigating allegations of non-criminal wrongdoing among OIG personnel, primarily managers.


Instructions: Read each scenario carefully. Select the single best response from the options provided. Responses are weighted on a 0 to 4-point scale. Select only one response per question.



1.
MINIMUM QUALIFICATIONS:

Please select the answer that best describes your experience as it directly relates to the GS-14 Administrative Investigations Specialist position. Carefully review your application to ensure that your response is supported by your resume. Please select only one answer.

Specialized experience at this level includes: • Managing, leading, planning, and coordinating Administrative Investigations to ensure inquiries are thoroughly reviewed, investigated, and resolved with efficiency and effectiveness. • Analyzing evidence and evaluating its sufficiency; making findings of fact, drawing conclusions regarding the propriety or advisability of action(s) based on requirements of law, rule, regulation, and/or standard practice. • Conducting analyses and preparing reports and presentations on findings and recommendations.
 I do not have the experience as described above.


2.
SCENARIO
While reviewing a newly submitted inquiry alleging that a supervisory OIG manager improperly redirected subordinates' work to benefit a personal contact, you notice the complaint contains internally inconsistent dates - the alleged conduct is described as occurring both before and after the manager joined the agency. The complainant appears credible on other dimensions.


What is your most appropriate first step?

 Flag it in the complaint summary as a critical inconsistency and contact the complainant to clarify the timeline.
 Proceed with a full inquiry and address the date issue if it becomes material to the findings.
 Close the inquiry administratively since the factual foundation appears unreliable.
 Accept the complaint as submitted and note the discrepancy in your final report.
 Refer the complaint back to the complainant without documenting your analysis.


3.
SCENARIO
You are preparing a complaint summary for DIG review regarding an OIG manager alleged to have retaliated against a subordinate for filing a grievance. In cross-referencing the subject's personnel file with the submitted evidence, you notice the subordinate's performance appraisal - cited as proof of retaliation - was signed three weeks before the grievance was filed, not after.


How do you handle this finding in your summary?
 Omit the discrepancy to avoid complicating the summary — it can be addressed during the full inquiry.
 Note the discrepancy in a footnote but retain the original allegation framing in the summary.
 Close the inquiry since the timeline undermines the allegation's viability.
 Explain the significance if the chronology of events to the retaliation theory and identify what additional evidence would be needed to evaluate the allegation.
 Flag it verbally to your supervisor without documenting it in the summary.


4.
SCENARIO
A DHS component submits an inquiry referral alleging a senior OIG manager engaged in a hostile work environment. The component's point of contact calls you directly asking for a status update two days after submission, expressing urgency because the alleged victim is considering leaving the agency.

What is the most appropriate response?

 Promise an expedited timeline given the urgency and proceed accordingly without supervisory approval.
 Advise the point of contact to have the alleged victim contact you directly.
 Share your preliminary assessment of the allegation's viability to reassure the contact.
 Decline to provide any information until the inquiry is formally opened.
 Explain the general intake timeline without disclosing deliberative details and document the contact.


5.
SCENARIO
During the intake of a complaint against an OIG manager, the complainant becomes frustrated and alleges that the OIG inquiry process is itself biased because it investigates its own personnel. They threaten to go to Congress directly if they feel their complaint is not taken seriously.

What is your most appropriate response?

 Assure the complainant the process is entirely unbiased and move forward with intake.
 Advise the complainant they are free to contact Congress and end the interaction.
 Explain the procedural safeguards that protect objectivity and the complainant's rights for reporting; document the interaction.
 Escalate immediately to your supervisor and let them handle the complainant.
 Note the complainant's hostility in the file as a credibility concern.


6.
SCENARIO
You have completed a management inquiry into allegations that an OIG supervisory official improperly awarded a performance bonus to a personal friend. Your findings are mixed - evidence supports some allegations but not others. The DIG has requested a final report with recommendations for corrective action.


How do you structure your report?

 Present all evidence collected and allow leadership to draw their own conclusions.
 Clearly state findings of fact for each with supporting evidence and limit corrective action recommendations to allegations that are substantiated.
 Focus the report on the substantiated allegations only and omit the unsubstantiated ones to keep the report concise.
 Draft a narrative summary without per-allegation conclusions and submit recommendations for a broader review.
 Report all allegations as substantiated to ensure corrective action is taken, noting the evidence gaps as caveats.


7.
SCENARIO
You are preparing a written briefing for senior OIG leadership recommending corrective action following a substantiated finding that a manager violated the agency's standards of conduct. The recommended action involves a significant personnel consequence. Your briefing will be the primary basis for leadership's decision.

What is essential to include in the briefing?
 A narrative of events with your personal opinion of the manager's character included to provide context.
 A recommendation for the most serious available sanction to signal the severity of the violation.
 The evidentiary record without a recommendation — it is leadership's role to decide.
 A summary of the investigation and your recommended corrective action without elaboration, trusting leadership to assess the basis.
 A clear statement of the substantiated violation, the evidentiary basis, and your specific recommendation with supporting rationale.


8.
SCENARIO
You are completing the MIP report for a referred inquiry that was ultimately closed without substantiation. The subject is an OIG manager with a clean disciplinary record. You draft a closing summary and realize you have used evaluative language characterizing the subject's behavior as 'suspicious' even though the allegation was unsubstantiated.

What do you do?

 Revise the summary to use neutral, fact-based language that accurately reflects the finding of non-substantiation.
 Retain the language since it reflects your professional judgment and may be useful if future allegations arise.
 Add a caveat noting that the language is your opinion rather than a finding.
 Submit the report as-is — the language is unlikely to be reviewed in detail since the inquiry is closing.
 Flag the issue to your supervisor before revising to get guidance on appropriate language standards.


9.
SCENARIO
You are leading an inquiry into allegations that an OIG manager manipulated timekeeping records for subordinates to conceal under-reported hours. The manager denies the allegations. The electronic timekeeping system shows edits were made, but the system logs attribute the edits to a shared administrative account used by multiple people.


How do you proceed?

 Conclude the allegation cannot be substantiated since the edits cannot be attributed to a specific individual and close the inquiry.
 Identify and interview all personnel with access to the shared account and request system audit logs to identify the actual editing user.
 Focus the inquiry on whether the timekeeping changes benefited the manager's subordinates as alleged and draw indirect inferences about responsibility.
 Request that IT forensically restore original records before the edits.
 Escalate to criminal investigators since the conduct may involve fraudulent record manipulation.


10.
SCENARIO
In reviewing witness statements collected during an inquiry, you find that three subordinates corroborate the allegation against the manager, but two senior employees offer accounts that directly contradict the subordinates. All witnesses appear credible. The manager denies the conduct.


What is the most appropriate analytical approach?

 Conclude the evidence is inconclusive and close the inquiry without a finding.
 Report the conflicting accounts to the DIG and request guidance on how to weigh them.
 Determine witness credibility though documentary evidence and proximity to the alleged conduct and document your assessment.
 Defer to the majority — three witnesses outweigh two, so the allegation is substantiated.
 Request additional witnesses to break the evidentiary deadlock.


11.
SCENARIO
While conducting a management inquiry, you discover evidence suggesting the manager under inquiry may also have engaged in conduct that could constitute a criminal offense (e.g., intentional falsification of official government records). This falls outside the scope of your administrative investigation.


What is the correct course of action?

 Contact the criminal investigative authority directly without informing your supervisor to avoid delay.
 Close the administrative inquiry immediately since criminal conduct supersedes your jurisdiction.
 Exclude the criminal conduct evidence from your inquiry file and focus solely on the administrative allegations.
 Preserve all relevant evidence, notify your supervisor, and prepare a referral to the appropriate criminal investigative unit.
 Continue the administrative inquiry and note the potential criminal conduct in the final report for leadership's consideration.


12.
SCENARIO
You are assigned to conduct a cadre training session for senior OIG managers who will be conducting management inquiries. During a role-play exercise, a manager-trainee reaches a conclusion that is factually supported by the scenario evidence but fails to document the regulatory standard violated - citing only that the conduct 'seemed wrong.'


How do you address this in the training?

 Ask another trainee to correct the error to foster peer learning.
 Pause the exercise, walk the trainee through identifying the applicable standard and caution against using subjective impressions.
 Allow the exercise to continue and debrief the entire group at the end.
 Note the error privately in the trainee's evaluation without addressing it in real time.
 Accept the conclusion since the factual finding is correct — regulatory citation is a secondary concern.


13.
SCENARIO
A DHS component submits a referral that is clearly outside your office's jurisdiction - it involves alleged misconduct by a federal employee who is not DHS personnel. The component believes your office handles all federal investigations.


What is your most appropriate action?

 Return the referral without explanation and await resubmission through the correct channel.
 Accept the referral and investigate it — the conduct appears serious enough to warrant action regardless of jurisdiction.
 Accept the referral and forward it to the correct office on the component's behalf without explanation.
 Decline the referral clearly and professionally, document the determination, and explain which office has jurisdiction.
 Log the referral as received, take no action, and close it administratively after 30 days.
