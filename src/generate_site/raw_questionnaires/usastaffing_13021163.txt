 Approve the program office’s rigid, single-contract strategy as submitted to ensure rapid administrative execution, advising the military services that they must adapt their field operations to match the contract’s limitations.

This fragmented approach has resulted in duplicated administrative costs, inconsistent service-level agreements, and a lack of enterprise visibility for the Director. How do you best guide the agency to satisfy this requirement at the enterprise level?

 Direct your division to draft a strict policy memo mandating that all future MSC/organization service requirements of this type be routed through HQ DLA for individual review and approval prior to solicitation.

To meet this aggressive timeline, the program office submits an acquisition strategy utilizing an obscure statutory authority that significantly stretches the boundaries of the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR). Your analysis indicates this approach carries a severe risk of sustained protests, which would ultimately delay the program far beyond the six-month deadline. As the Chief of Acquisition Operations, how do you best advise the Head of Contracting Activity (HCA) and the Director?

 Immediately reject the program office's strategy as non-compliant and mandate that they restart the planning process using standard, lengthy FAR Part 15 procedures, regardless of the impact on the Director's congressional commitment.

 Request an immediate, private briefing with the HCA and Program Executive. Clearly outline the severe protest risks that threaten the Director's timeline, and proactively present a legally sound, rapid-execution alternative—such as utilizing existing multi-agency IDIQs or a phased rollout approach—that achieves the six-month goal while maintaining compliance.
