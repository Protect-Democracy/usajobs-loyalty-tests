 Ocean Warming and Coral Bleaching: Elevated sea surface temperatures cause corals to expel the symbiotic algae (zooxanthellae) living in their tissues, a process known as coral bleaching. While corals can recover from brief bleaching events, prolonged or severe heat (such as the unprecedented 2023 marine heat wave) often leads to mass mortality.
 Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease (SCTLD): This is a highly lethal marine disease outbreak that has affected more than 20 species of Florida's reef-building corals. The disease often coincides with or is made worse by environmental stressors like warmer water temperatures.
 Land-Based Pollution: Runoff from agriculture, urban areas, and inadequately treated wastewater and sewage introduces excess nutrients, sediment, and pollutants (including certain sunscreen chemicals) into coastal waters. This pollution fuels algal growth that smothers corals and makes them more susceptible to disease.
 Ocean Acidification: As oceans absorb more carbon dioxide from the atmosphere, the water becomes more acidic, making it harder for corals and other calcifying organisms to build their skeletons and shells.
