This assessment presents a series of work-related scenarios. For each scenario, you are given five possible courses of action (A-E). While more than one option may seem reasonable, you must select the single best response that you believe would be the most effective and appropriate action to take in that situation.


2.
A contractor submits an invoice that includes charges for work that appears to be outside the agreed-upon scope of the contract. What is your first step?

 Pay the invoice to maintain a good relationship with the contractor and ask about the charges later.
 Reject the entire invoice immediately and notify the contractor that the charges are disputed.
 Ask the project manager if the extra work was verbally approved without checking the contract.
 Review the contract's statement of work and terms, document the specific discrepancies, and then contact the contractor to request clarification and justification for the charges.


3.
During a project, you discover that a critical government-furnished material will be delivered two months late, potentially delaying the entire project timeline and increasing costs. What is the most effective course of action?

 Analyze the contract for clauses related to delays and remedies, notify all stakeholders of the potential impact, and facilitate a discussion to explore mitigation strategies, such as sourcing an alternative or adjusting the project schedule.
 Wait for the contractor to report the issue, as it is their responsibility to manage the project schedule.
 Immediately direct the contractor to find an alternative material, regardless of the potential cost increase.
 Inform your supervisor that the project will be late and that there is nothing that can be done.


4.
A contractor's performance has been consistently declining, failing to meet several key performance indicators (KPIs) outlined in the Quality Assurance Surveillance Plan (QASP). How should you address this?

 Schedule a meeting with the contractor to express your disappointment and ask them to improve their performance.
 Start withholding payments to motivate the contractor to improve their performance immediately.
 Formally document all instances of non-compliance against the QASP, issue a Cure Notice specifying the failures and required remedies, and schedule a formal review to discuss a corrective action plan.
 Begin searching for a new contractor to take over the work, assuming the current one cannot be fixed.


5.
Your cross-functional team has just worked long hours to successfully finalize a complex and difficult contract award ahead of a tight deadline. How do you handle the aftermath?
 Publicly acknowledge the team's effort, highlighting specific contributions from individual members, and celebrate the milestone to reinforce team pride and mutual trust.
 Send a quick "thank you" email to the group and immediately begin assigning tasks for the next project.
 Only formally recognize the most senior members or leads of the team for their guidance.
 Accept the praise from upper management on behalf of the project without mentioning the specific efforts of your team members.


6.
You are leading a newly formed group of Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) and contracting personnel who have never worked together before. How do you build a strong group identity and commitment?

 Assign tasks immediately and let everyone work independently, assuming their professionalism will naturally bring them together over time.
 Host a kickoff meeting to collaboratively establish shared goals, clearly define how each person's role contributes to the mission, and create a shared agreement on how the team will communicate.
 Keep the SMEs and the contracting personnel siloed to ensure they don't step on each other's toes.
 Schedule a casual team lunch so people can meet, but leave the project expectations to be figured out as work progresses.


7.
Two key team members are repeatedly clashing over how to evaluate a contractor's proposed technical solution, which is lowering team morale and stalling progress. What is your approach?

 Ignore the conflict, as professionals should be able to work out their own disagreements.
 Make the final decision yourself to end the argument quickly and get the team back on track.
 Tell them privately that their arguing is unprofessional and order them to compromise immediately.
 Facilitate a collaborative discussion with both individuals to understand their perspectives, find common ground based on the contract requirements, and align them back to the team's overall goal.


8.
You notice a recurring pattern where an internal customer (the program office) frequently submits incomplete requirement packages, which constantly delays the procurement process. How do you address this?

 Fix the minor errors yourself each time to save the customer the hassle and keep the process moving.
 Complain to your supervisor about the program office's inability to submit correct paperwork.
 Proactively develop a standardized, easy-to-use checklist and offer a short, constructive training session to the program office to improve the quality of future submissions.
 Routinely reject the packages with a link to the regulations, waiting for them to figure out how to do it correctly.


9.
An internal customer urgently requests a specific brand of equipment that is currently unavailable through existing contracts. However, they need a solution to meet an upcoming operational deadline. What is your best response?

 Proactively research alternative equipment that meets their core operational needs and present them with viable, compliant procurement options that can be executed within their timeframe.
 Direct them to use a Government Purchase Card (GPC) to buy it commercially without checking if it violates any acquisition regulations.
 Inform them that the exact brand is unavailable and wait for them to suggest a different brand.
 Tell them the request is impossible to fulfill because the item isn't on the current contract.


10.
A customer demands that you expedite a critical contract modification by skipping a required peer review process, citing extreme operational urgency. How do you handle this request?

 Agree to skip the review this one time to deliver high-quality customer service and keep the customer happy.
 Refuse the request flatly, stating that acquisition regulations can never be bypassed under any circumstances.
 Acknowledge their urgency, explain that the review is necessary to ensure the modification's quality and legal compliance, and offer to personally shepherd the package through the review board to minimize any delay.
 Tell the review board to just approve the package without actually reading it to save time.


11.
You need to brief a senior leader on a complex contract issue that has significant financial and legal implications. What is the best approach for the briefing?

 Start with a concise executive summary ("bottom line up front") of the issue, its impact, and your recommended course of action, followed by supporting details and a Q&A session.
 Present a detailed, chronological history of the contract from the beginning to ensure the leader has all possible background information first.
 Provide a large binder of all relevant documents and let the leader ask questions as they review them.
 Focus the briefing on the mistakes made by others that led to the current issue.


12.
During a contentious post-award conference, the contractor's team becomes defensive and argumentative about your interpretation of a performance standard. How should you respond?

 Maintain a calm and professional tone, acknowledge their concern, and redirect the conversation back to the specific language in the contract. Suggest a follow-up meeting focused solely on that standard to ensure mutual understanding without derailing the current meeting.
 Respond with equal aggression to show that you will not be intimidated.
 End the meeting immediately and reschedule for when tempers have cooled.
 Concede the point to avoid conflict and keep the meeting moving forward.


13.
You are asked to give an impromptu status update on your project at a large meeting. You don't have all the specific data points memorized. What do you do?

 Apologize and state that you cannot provide an update without your notes.
 Try to recall as many specific numbers as you can, even if you are not sure they are accurate.
 Provide a high-level summary of the project's status, progress, and any major challenges, and state that you will follow up with the group via email with precise data points after the meeting.
 Divert the question by talking about a different topic where you feel more confident.


14.
You need to write a letter to a contractor terminating a contract for cause due to poor performance. What is the most critical element to include in the letter?

 A general statement that the contractor's performance was unsatisfactory.
 A detailed emotional account of the frustration the project team has experienced.
 A notification that the contract is terminated, effective immediately, with no further details.
 A clear and objective statement of the termination, citing the specific contract clauses that authorize it, referencing previously issued cure notices, and detailing the factual, documented instances of non-compliance.


15.
You are preparing a monthly status report for project stakeholders. The report should be:

 As long and detailed as possible, including every email and meeting minute from the past month.
 Brief and high-level, omitting any mention of risks or problems to avoid worrying stakeholders.
 A concise summary of progress against the project plan, an overview of the budget, a transparent update on risks and mitigation efforts, and a clear outlook for the next reporting period.

16.
When preparing the statement of work (SOW) for a new acquisition, what is the most important principle to follow in your writing?

 Write in clear, unambiguous language that defines specific, measurable, and enforceable outcomes and deliverables, leaving no room for misinterpretation.
 Use vague and flexible language to allow the contractor to propose innovative solutions you haven't thought of.
 Incorporate complex legal and technical jargon to demonstrate the government's expertise.
 Copy and paste the entire SOW from a previous, unrelated contract to save time.
