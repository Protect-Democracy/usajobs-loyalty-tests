To be creditable, specialized experience must be at the GS-14 grade level or equivalent under other pay systems in the Federal service, military or private sector. Creditable specialized experience includes managing complex operations involving foreign military sales cases; serving as the principle for country specific security cooperation issues on foreign military sales; and participating in actions involving foreign military sales, leases, and grants to foreign governments.

2.
As a team leader for junior Country Portfolio Analysts, what is your primary role?

 To manage the budget for the entire team.
 To serve as a mentor and guide for their professional development.
 To assign daily tasks and monitor their completion.
 To conduct performance reviews exclusively.


3.
When handling country-specific issues, the incumbent serves as the principle Point of Contact (POC). What does this entail?

 Only reporting issues to senior management.
 Providing leadership, policy guidance, and ensuring corrective action.
 Deferring all decisions to the Department of State.
 Managing logistical support for in-country teams.


4.
Under which legal authorities are sales, leases, and grants to foreign governments managed?

 Title 5 and Title 15 authorities.
 Title 10 and Title 22 authorities.
 The Foreign Assistance Act exclusively.
 The Arms Export Control Act exclusively.


5.
What is the incumbent's role regarding Exceptions to the National Disclosure Policy (ENDP)?

 To personally approve or deny all ENDP actions.
 To draft the initial policy for all ENDP cases.
 To review, coordinate, and facilitate information to ensure prompt responses.
 To report ENDP actions to the media.


6.
When a Presidential drawdown of defense articles is authorized for a designated country, what is the analyst's responsibility?

 To select the articles and services to be drawn down.
 To lead the transportation and delivery of the items.
 To manage the coordination, execution, and reconciliation of the drawdown.
 To negotiate the financial terms with the recipient country.


7.
The incumbent serves as a focal point for the preparation, coordination, negotiations, and implementation of what documents?

 Internal DSCA personnel policies.
 Contracts with private industry vendors.
 International Agreements, MOAs, and MOUs.
 Annual budget proposals for the agency.


8.
Who prepares the lease documentation that the Country Portfolio Analyst reviews and analyzes?

 The foreign government's Ministry of Defense.
 The Military Departments (MILDEPs).
 The Department of State (DoS).
 The Defense Security Cooperation Agency (DSCA) legal team.


9.
What is the analyst's role concerning Excess Defense Articles (EDA) for their assigned countries?

 To determine the final price of the EDA.
 To personally inspect all EDA before transfer.
 To monitor requests and facilitate allocations and notifications.
 To decide which countries are eligible for EDA.


10.
In supporting major arms sales, what is a key function related to the U.S. Congress?

 To vote on the approval of the sale.
 To lobby members of Congress to support the sale.
 To review data for Congressional notifications and support briefings for Members and Staff.
 To provide funding directly to Congressional committees.


11.
What specific training programs does the analyst help monitor for their assigned countries?

 Basic military recruit training.
 Civilian professional development courses.
 IMET and Expand-IMET programs.
 Language and cultural immersion programs.

The analyst serves as a focal point for the allocation and execution of what type of grant funds?

 Foreign Military Financing (FMF) exclusively.
 Economic Support Funds (ESF).
 Non-FMF grant funds like NADR and PKO.
 Development Assistance (DA) funds.


13.

 Representatives from non-governmental organizations (NGOs).
 Officials from the foreign government.

 Defense contractors from U.S. industry.


14.
In the context of Letters of Offer and Acceptance (LOAs), what is a key responsibility of the analyst?

 To determine the final price and availability on all LOAs.
 To sign the LOA on behalf of the U.S. Government.
 To ensure the LOA complies with policies like the SAMM, FAA, and AECA.
 To translate the LOA into the partner nation's language.
