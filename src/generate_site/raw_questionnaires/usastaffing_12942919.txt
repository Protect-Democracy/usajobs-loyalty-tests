During a boarding of a fishing vessel at sea, you conduct an initial security sweep to identify potential threats to the boarding team. While moving through the main cabin, you observe multiple rectangular blocks wrapped in clear cellophane sitting on a workbench. You also observe five additional identical bundles in the fish hold. A field test of the substance returns a positive result for illegal narcotics. You arrest the captain and crew. Was this a lawful search?
 No. The search was unlawful because the Plain View Doctrine does not apply to maritime boardings; a federal search warrant must be obtained before any evidence found during a security sweep can be legally seized.
 Yes. The discovery of the evidence was lawful under the Plain View Doctrine because the items were encountered from a lawful vantage point during a valid security sweep, and their incriminating nature was immediately apparent.
 Yes. The search was valid because the boarding was initiated based on a verified tip that the captain was smuggling drugs across the border, granting the team the authority to inspect all areas of the vessel.
 No. The search was invalid because a protective security sweep is strictly limited to the detection of hidden persons; any contraband observed while performing the sweep is considered "fruit of the poisonous tree" and is inadmissible.
