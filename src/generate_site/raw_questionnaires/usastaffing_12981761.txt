Full implementation is required within 180 days. Your initial review identifies three problems: EXIM's current IT infrastructure cannot support the monitoring requirements without a capital investment that exceeds your office's budget authority; two operational offices have raised objections citing employee privacy concerns and potential union obligations; and a sister agency that received the same directive has already begun implementation using an approach your legal counsel believes may not be Privacy Act compliant. Leadership wants to know your implementation plan within two weeks. What would you MOST likely do first?
 Brief leadership on the infrastructure gap and request additional budget authority before beginning any implementation planning — proceeding without confirmed resources risks a failed rollout and credibility loss for the program.
 Adopt the sister agency's implementation model to accelerate your timeline, since another federal agency's approach provides reasonable cover and reduces the risk of being non-compliant with the ICD.
 Engage the two objecting offices and your union representative first to resolve labor relations concerns before bringing a plan to leadership, since unresolved opposition will undermine implementation regardless of the compliance deadline.
 Develop a phased implementation strategy that maps ICD requirements to current gaps, coordinates with IT, legal, HR, Privacy Act, and labor obligations, identifies interim compensating measures to demonstrate compliance momentum, and briefs leadership with a realistic timeline and resource requirements — escalating the budget gap as a formal risk requiring senior decision.
