For each one, select the response that best reflects how you would handle the situation. Each question has five response options. These range from the most appropriate to the least appropriate action, but the options are presented in random order. Your task is to choose the option you believe is the best course of action in a federal workplace setting. There are no right or wrong answers. Your responses should reflect sound judgment and professional decision-making. This test evaluates how you approach workplace situations, not your prior experience or background. Please answer honestly and thoughtfully. Your responses will help determine your qualifications for this position.

You are developing a Project Management Plan (PMP) for a complex facility with a mandated funding deadline. The Project Delivery Team's (PDT) technical lead reports an 18-month design estimate, but the customer's representative on the PDT is advocating for a 12-month schedule. As the Program Manager, how do you best align the PMP to optimize project parameters and address this discrepancy?

 Engage the engineering team to identify critical path activities that can be fast-tracked with added resources, while formally documenting the associated risks to quality and safety standards for inclusion in the Project Management Plan (PMP).
 Convene the Project Delivery Team (PDT) to conduct a risk analysis on the compressed schedule, develop viable alternatives with phased options and parallel work streams, and present these options to the customer for a collaborative decision.

 Accept the 12-month timeline by directing the team to curtail design reviews and safety assessments, asserting that congressional funding deadlines supersede established USACE quality and safety protocols in the Project Management Plan (PMP).
 Propose a compromise 15-month schedule by overlapping non-critical design activities and formally present this single alternative to the customer along with a high-level overview of the associated risks and potential cost impacts.
