Assessment 1
This assessment presents 12 scenario-based situational judgment questions designed to evaluate candidates' competency across seven major duty areas identified in the Job Analysis for the GS-15 Senior Advisor, Office of Integrity position. The position functions as a principal advisor directly supporting the Deputy Inspector General / Assistant Inspector General, with enterprise-facing responsibilities spanning policy development, program oversight, strategic planning, and executive advisory services across OIG investigative and oversight functions.


Instructions: Read each scenario carefully. Select the single response that best reflects effective judgment for a GS-15 senior advisory role. Each response is weighted on a 0-4 point scale. Select only one response per question. The best answer is not always the same letter.

2.
SCENARIO
You have been asked by the Deputy Inspector General to lead an enterprise-wide policy review assessing whether current investigative protocols align with recent changes in DHS statutory authority. Three OIG components have submitted conflicting interpretations of the same regulatory provision, each with supporting legal rationale. Component heads are firmly committed to their respective positions. The DIG expects a consolidated advisory recommendation within two weeks.

What is your most appropriate course of action?
 Convene a working group with representatives from each component, consolidate the competing interpretations into a single briefing document, and present all three positions to the DIG for a final determination without recommending a preferred approach.
 Conduct an independent analysis of the statutory text, agency precedent, and relevant OPM and DOJ guidance; identify where the competing interpretations diverge and why; and develop a single, well-reasoned advisory recommendation to the DIG with supporting rationale and a proposed implementation path.
 Defer to the component with the longest-established interpretation on the grounds of organizational precedent, and recommend the DIG adopt that position to maintain consistency with past practice.
 Request a 30-day extension from the DIG to conduct a full external legal review before committing to any recommendation, ensuring the advisory product has maximum defensibility.
 Align the recommendation with whichever component interpretation is most operationally convenient, then develop policy language broad enough to accommodate all three positions without explicitly resolving the conflict.


3.
SCENARIO
The Inspector General has directed your office to evaluate the effectiveness of the OIG's current case prioritization framework, which has been in place for six years. Preliminary data suggests backlogs have grown by 40% in two years, but component directors attribute the increase to resource constraints rather than framework deficiencies. You are responsible for producing an evaluation report with recommendations.


How do you approach the evaluation?
 Accept the component directors' attribution of the backlog to resource constraints, frame your report around a resource augmentation recommendation, and note that the framework itself is functioning as designed.
 Limit the evaluation to components that have voluntarily agreed to participate in order to preserve working relationships and avoid creating adversarial dynamics with component leadership.
 Design an evaluation methodology that independently tests both hypotheses — framework deficiency and resource constraint — using case-level data, workflow analysis, and benchmarking against comparable oversight organizations, then allow the evidence to determine which conclusion the report supports.
 Design an evaluation that independently tests both the framework and resource variables using case-level data, workflow analysis, and peer organization benchmarks; assess root causes with quantitative and qualitative rigor; and produce findings with differentiated recommendations addressing both structural and resource dimensions where the evidence supports each.
 Conduct individual interviews with all component directors to build a consensus narrative about causes, then synthesize their input into an evaluation report that reflects the most commonly expressed views.


4.
SCENARIO
During a senior leadership offsite, you learn that DHS is developing a department-wide initiative that will significantly restructure how component OIGs coordinate on cross-jurisdictional investigations. The initiative is being led by a DHS policy office with limited OIG subject-matter expertise, and the proposed framework contains provisions that would inadvertently restrict OIG independence as defined under the Inspector General Act. The initiative is in a late draft stage and is scheduled for publication in 45 days.


What is your most strategically appropriate response?

 Prepare a detailed written objection for the record, route it through your chain of command for IG signature and submit it formally to the DHS policy office as a matter of institutional concern.
 Wait for the initiative to be published, then prepare a formal response identifying the provisions that conflict with the Inspector General Act and recommend corrective action through the appropriate oversight channels.
 Immediately brief the IG and DIG on the specific provisions creating independence risk, prepare a targeted technical assistance document identifying the legal conflicts and proposing alternative language, and proactively engage the DHS policy office to resolve the issues before publication — while coordinating with the Council of Inspectors General on Integrity and Efficiency (CIGIE) if broader IG community equities are implicated.
 Engage informally with a DHS policy office contact to flag the concern, suggest they consult their own legal counsel, and allow the process to proceed while monitoring for any final-stage corrections.
 Identify which provisions can be managed through operational workarounds within the OIG without formally challenging the initiative, and prepare internal guidance for staff on how to maintain independence within the new framework's constraints.


5.
SCENARIO
The OIG is preparing a congressionally mandated report on the effectiveness of DHS's border security technology investments. You are the senior advisor leading the effort. Three weeks before the deadline, your team discovers that a key data set provided by DHS CBP - which underlies several of the report's central findings - contains a significant coding error that inflates performance metrics by approximately 18%. Correcting the data will require renegotiating assumptions with CBP and potentially revising the report's conclusions.


What is your most appropriate course of action?
 Proceed with the original data set, add a technical footnote disclosing the known limitation, and note that corrected data will be reflected in the next reporting cycle to avoid missing the congressional deadline.
 Immediately notify your chain of command, engage CBP to obtain corrected data, assess the full impact of the error on the report's findings, and communicate proactively with congressional staff about a short extension if the corrected findings materially alter the report's conclusions — prioritizing accuracy over administrative convenience.
 Brief the IG and DIG immediately on the data integrity issue and its implications; work in parallel to obtain corrected data from CBP, quantify the impact on each affected finding, revise conclusions where warranted, and coordinate with OIG legislative affairs to inform congressional staff of the situation and any timeline adjustment — ensuring the final product is both accurate and appropriately transparent.
 Identify which report sections are most directly affected by the error, limit revisions to those sections only, and submit the remainder of the report on schedule with a notation that a supplemental addendum addressing the data correction will follow within 30 days.
 Request that CBP quietly correct the data without documenting the error or its discovery, rerun your analysis using the corrected figures, and submit the report on time without disclosing the interim data integrity issue.

SCENARIO
You have developed a comprehensive enterprise-wide policy reform proposal that would standardize investigative quality review processes across all OIG components. The proposal has the IG's conceptual support. However, three component heads - whose buy-in is essential for successful implementation - are resistant. One objects on resource grounds, one believes the proposal encroaches on component autonomy, and one has technical concerns about the proposed metrics.


How do you most effectively advance the proposal?

 Present the proposal at a full senior leadership meeting and rely on the IG's endorsement to compel adoption, making clear that implementation is expected regardless of component-level objections.
 Meet individually with each resistant component head to understand the specific nature of their concerns, adapt the proposal's implementation design where their objections have merit without compromising the policy's core objectives, build a coalition of supportive components to demonstrate momentum, and develop a phased rollout that addresses the resource concern — then present a revised proposal that reflects the negotiated adjustments.
 Refer the matter to the IG to resolve the component-level resistance directly and resubmit the proposal only after the IG has addressed each objection with the relevant component head.
 Modify the proposal to make the quality review framework voluntary for resistant components in the first implementation phase, then use early adopter results as evidence to encourage broader participation over time.
 Withdraw the proposal temporarily, convene a working group co-chaired by the resistant component heads to redesign the framework collaboratively, and resubmit after the working group reaches consensus on a revised approach.

SCENARIO
You learn through informal channels that a DHS component is planning a significant internal reorganization that will directly affect the OIG's jurisdictional scope for oversight of two major programs. The reorganization has not been formally communicated to the OIG, and there is no indication that OIG equities were considered during the planning process. The reorganization is reportedly three months from implementation.


What is your most appropriate action?

 Document what you have learned through informal channels and present the issue to the IG at the next scheduled leadership meeting, recommending that the OIG request a formal briefing from the component.
 Reach out directly to your counterpart at the DHS component to gather details informally, then determine whether the OIG's oversight equities are actually at risk before escalating internally.
 Brief the IG and DIG immediately on what you have learned, assess the potential jurisdictional and operational implications using available program documentation, and recommend that the OIG proactively engage DHS leadership at the appropriate level to ensure OIG equities are considered before the reorganization is finalized — coordinating with OIG legal counsel and legislative affairs as warranted.
 Prepare a formal written analysis of the jurisdictional implications and route it through your chain of command before taking any external action, to ensure the OIG's position is fully developed before engaging the component.
 Monitor the situation through existing interagency channels and wait for formal communication from the DHS component before taking any action, to avoid acting on unverified information.


8.
SCENARIO
During a contentious interagency working group meeting that you are facilitating, a senior DHS component official interrupts a colleague's presentation, dismisses the OIG's analytical methodology as 'fundamentally flawed,' and accuses the OIG of operating with an institutional bias in its oversight approach. Several other participants appear uncomfortable, and the meeting is in danger of breaking down. You are the senior OIG representative in the room.


What is your most appropriate response in the moment?

 Firmly defend the OIG's methodology on the merits, directly challenge the official's characterization of institutional bias, and make clear that the OIG's independence precludes external criticism of its analytical approach.
 Acknowledge the official's concerns, suggest that the methodological discussion would benefit from a separate technical session, redirect the meeting to its primary agenda to restore a productive working environment, and follow up bilaterally with the official after the meeting to address their specific concerns substantively.
 Pause the meeting, acknowledge that the official has raised concerns worth addressing, invite a brief and structured clarification of the specific methodological objections, respond on the merits where possible, commit to providing written follow-up on any points requiring further analysis, and then re-establish meeting norms before continuing.
 Calmly acknowledge the official's concern without conceding the point, temporarily pause the discussion of the contested material, ensure the remaining participants have an opportunity to contribute productively, close the meeting constructively, and promptly brief the IG and DIG on the exchange — including a recommendation on whether and how to address the methodological challenge substantively and the relationship with this component going forward.
 Defer immediately to the official by acknowledging that the methodology may benefit from revision, offer to restructure the OIG's analytical approach to better align with component expectations, and propose a joint working group to develop a mutually acceptable framework.

SCENARIO
You are advising the IG on resource allocation for the upcoming fiscal year. Budget projections show a 12% reduction in discretionary spending. Two major enterprise initiatives - a technology modernization program and an expansion of the sensitive investigations cadre - are both at critical implementation stages and cannot both be fully funded. Component directors are lobbying independently for their respective priorities. The IG has asked for your recommendation.


How do you approach developing the recommendation?

 A Conduct a structured comparative analysis of both initiatives using criteria including statutory obligations, mission-criticality, sunk cost, risk of deferral, and organizational readiness; model multiple budget scenarios showing the cost-benefit of full funding, partial funding, and deferral for each; and present the IG with a recommended allocation and the tradeoff analysis that supports it, with contingency options if circumstances change.
 Survey component directors to determine which initiative has the broadest base of internal support, weight your recommendation toward that initiative to build organizational buy-in, and present the outcome as reflecting leadership consensus.
 Recommend splitting the reduction equally between both initiatives, preserving partial momentum on each rather than fully defunding either, and flag the funding shortfall in both programs for leadership consideration in the next budget cycle.
 Recommend deferring the initiative with the lower sunk cost, document your rationale, and advise the IG to communicate the decision to affected stakeholders with clear messaging about the factors that drove the prioritization.
 Recommend convening a formal prioritization panel of component directors to reach a consensus decision before presenting any recommendation to the IG, ensuring the final allocation has broad organizational legitimacy.

SCENARIO
You are reviewing a draft of the OIG's next four-year strategic plan. The plan's objectives are largely forward-looking but fail to address a pattern you have observed over the past 18 months: several OIG recommendations to DHS components have gone unimplemented, and there is no tracking mechanism or escalation protocol for sustained non-compliance. The draft plan also does not address how the OIG will measure the impact of its work beyond outputs.


What is your most appropriate advisory response to the draft plan?

 Annotate the draft with your observations and route it back to the planning team for revision, recommending they add a recommendation-tracking section and an outcome measurement framework before the next review cycle.
 Raise the recommendation-tracking gap informally with the plan's author and suggest they consult OIG components to determine whether a tracking mechanism is operationally feasible before including it in the plan.
 Prepare a supplemental advisory memorandum for the IG and DIG that identifies the two structural gaps — recommendation implementation tracking and outcome measurement — explains their strategic significance, provides examples of how comparable oversight bodies have addressed both issues, and recommends specific additions to the plan including a draft escalation protocol and a proposed outcome metrics framework.
 Accept the plan as drafted on the grounds that strategic plans are inherently high-level and implementation details including tracking mechanisms are appropriately addressed through operational planning at the component level.
 Flag the two gaps in the next senior leadership meeting and recommend the IG establish a working group to develop both a tracking mechanism and an outcome framework as separate workstreams outside the strategic plan.

SCENARIO
You are representing the OIG in a joint working group tasked with developing a department-wide policy on the use of artificial intelligence in investigative case management. Representatives from DHS legal, privacy, and technology offices are advocating for an expansive AI implementation framework that would give DHS program offices significant control over AI tools used in OIG investigative workflows. You believe this structure would compromise OIG independence and investigative integrity.


What is your most effective approach in this working group?

 State the OIG's position clearly at the outset, decline to participate in working group discussions that could be interpreted as endorsing the proposed framework, and submit the OIG's formal objections in writing for the record.
 Participate constructively in the working group while building relationships with the privacy and legal office representatives, identify shared interests around data integrity and legal compliance, develop coalition support for an amended framework that preserves OIG operational control over its own AI tools, and present the revised framework with legal and operational rationale that addresses the other participants' core interests.
 Agree to the proposed framework in the working group to preserve the collaborative relationship, then work through separate channels to negotiate carve-outs for OIG-specific investigative tools after the broader policy is finalized.
 Request that the IG formally recuse the OIG from the working group and engage at the DHS Deputy Secretary level, on the grounds that the proposed framework raises separation-of-function concerns that cannot be resolved in a staff-level forum.
 Propose that the working group create a carve-out subcommittee specifically for oversight body participation, allowing the OIG to develop its own AI governance framework in parallel with — but separate from — the department-wide policy.

SCENARIO
During your review of a sensitive internal OIG matter referred to you by the DIG, you identify information suggesting that a recently completed OIG audit - which received significant public attention and was cited in congressional testimony - may have relied on an analytical methodology that does not withstand current scrutiny. The audit was led by a respected senior official who has since retired. Reopening the question has significant reputational and institutional implications.


What is your most appropriate course of action?
 Document your concerns and present them to the DIG with a recommendation for a quiet internal methodology review, framed as a quality assurance exercise rather than a formal reopening, to minimize institutional exposure.
 Prepare a detailed written analysis of the methodological concerns, present it directly to the IG — not only the DIG — given the reputational and institutional stakes, and recommend a structured, independent internal review with a clear protocol for how findings will be handled if the methodology is confirmed to be deficient, including consideration of whether correction or disclosure obligations exist.
 Seek an informal second opinion from a trusted peer with audit expertise before raising the issue internally, to determine whether your methodological concern is well-founded before potentially triggering a significant institutional response.
 Raise the concern with the DIG as instructed, but recommend taking no further action given the retired official's reputation and the potential damage that a public disclosure of methodological deficiency could cause to the OIG's credibility.
 Refer the matter to the OIG's Office of General Counsel for an independent determination of whether the methodological concern rises to the level requiring formal action, and defer to their guidance before making any recommendation to leadership.

SCENARIO
You are the senior advisor supporting a cross-component OIG task force examining systemic vulnerabilities in DHS's grant oversight functions. The task force has collected extensive data but analysis is stalled because two components are interpreting a key legal authority differently, producing incompatible analytical frameworks that prevent the group from reaching coherent conclusions. The task force report is due to the IG in six weeks.


What is your most effective approach to resolving the analytical impasse?
 Refer the legal interpretation question to OIG legal counsel for a formal opinion, pause the analytical work until the opinion is received, and restructure the timeline to accommodate the delay.
 Direct each component to proceed with its own analytical framework and produce separate findings sections in the report, with a synthesis section identifying where the differing interpretations lead to different conclusions and what the implications are for each.
 Convene the two component leads in a facilitated session to negotiate a shared working interpretation that both can accept, frame it as a task force operational definition rather than a legal determination, and proceed with the analysis under that shared definition.
 Escalate the legal interpretation dispute to the IG and DIG and request they resolve it through a direct directive to the relevant components, removing the interpretive discretion from the task force entirely.
 Identify the specific points of interpretive divergence, prepare a structured legal analysis memo presenting the strongest case for each interpretation along with applicable precedent and OPM or DOJ guidance, and present the memo to legal counsel with a request for an expedited opinion — while simultaneously designing the analytical framework to be modular so that conclusions can be finalized quickly once the legal question is resolved.
