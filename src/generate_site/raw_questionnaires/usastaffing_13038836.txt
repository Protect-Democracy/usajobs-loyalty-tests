3.
Are you a current or former federal employee displaced from the Department of Justice?

The Career Transition Assistance Plan (CTAP) is an intra-agency program that helps surplus or displaced federal employees improve their chances of finding a new job in their agency, by giving them selection priority over other applicants, as long as they're qualified for the job.

To be eligible under CTAP you must meet all of the following:
You are located in the same local commuting area of this vacancy; AND
You are applying to a position that is at (or below) the grade level of the position from which you were (or will) be separated from; AND
You are applying to a position that does not have greater promotion potential than the position from which you were (or will be) separated from; AND
Your last performance rating of record is at least fully successful, or the equivalent.
To verify your eligibility: You must submit appropriate supporting documentation, such as a Reduction in Force (RIF) separation notice or a Proposed Removal/Separation notice to include your latest performance rating (if the notice does not have this information included, you must submit a copy of your most recent performance appraisal) and a copy of your latest Notification of Personnel Action (SF-50). Time-in-Grade Requirements

Current or former Federal employees must meet time-in-grade requirements for consideration. A Notification of Personnel Action, SF-50, showing your time-in-grade eligibility must be submitted with your application materials.

If you are a current or former Federal employee, you must provide your most recent UNREDACTED SF-50 to determine proof of status and time-in-grade eligibility. If the most recent SF-50 has an effective date within the past year, it may not clearly demonstrate you possess one-year time-in-grade, as required by the announcement. In this instance, you must provide an additional SF-50 that clearly demonstrates one-year time-in-grade.

Award SF-50's do not provide sufficient information and will not be accepted for the purpose of verifying time-in-grade.

Examples of appropriate SF-50's include:
Within grade increases at the highest grade held; or
Promotions with an effective date more than one year old; or
SF-50's at the highest grade held with an effective date more than one year old.

This assessment measures the technical competencies required for the Captain position. For each question, select the answer that best represents the most effective and appropriate approach. Only one answer per question will be scored.


1.
The Bureau of Prisons has just released a significant revision to the Use of Force policy that will fundamentally change how correctional staff must document and report incidents. As Captain, you have been tasked with ensuring that all correctional staff at your institution understand and can properly implement these new procedures. The Executive Staff (Warden, Associate Wardens, and Department Heads) will be meeting tomorrow morning, and you have been asked to present the new policy changes and explain how they will impact daily operations. You need to ensure leadership understands the policy's implications for training, documentation, supervisory review, and potential liability issues.

Which action represents the most effective and appropriate oral communication approach in this situation?
 Conduct a briefing for the Executive Staff meeting where you present a comprehensive overview of the new Use of Force policy revisions, explain the operational and legal implications for the institution, provide guidance on implementation strategies, outline the training requirements for all staff levels, and address questions about how the policy changes will affect current procedures and practices.
 Schedule and facilitate a meeting with your Lieutenant staff to discuss the new Use of Force policy, explain the basic requirements to your direct subordinates, and answer their questions about how to properly apply the policy in routine situations.
 Participate in the Executive Staff meeting by providing technical information about the specific documentation requirements in the new policy when the Warden asks for your input on budgetary or staffing implications.
 Prepare detailed written handouts about the new Use of Force policy that Executive Staff members can review on their own time, and make yourself available to answer questions if they arise after they have read the materials.

The Warden has requested a detailed written report of your findings, as the U.S. Attorney's office may use this information to determine whether criminal charges should be filed against the truck driver and a food service employee. The report must clearly document the chain of events, evidence collected, witness statements, and your professional conclusions.

 Review the incident reports submitted by subordinate officers who assisted with the investigation, make corrections to their grammar and formatting, and forward the corrected reports to the Warden with a cover memo.
 Create a standardized form letter template that can be used for future contraband investigations, outlining the general procedures followed and typical outcomes of such cases.

 Conduct a security audit of the tool control, key control, and perimeter security systems, document your findings in a report, and provide training to staff on how to properly respond to security breaches in these areas should they occur.
