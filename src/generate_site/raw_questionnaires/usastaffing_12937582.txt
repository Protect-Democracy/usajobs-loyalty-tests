2.
A major duty is to "identify, analyze, coordinate, and resolves logistics support shortfalls" for nuclear weapon systems. If you identified a critical support shortfall for a key NC3 system component, what would be your immediate course of action?

 Document the shortfall in a report and submit it through normal channels for the next quarterly review.
 Immediately form an ad-hoc Integrated Product Team (IPT) with members from the relevant DLA supply chain and AFNWC program office, analyze the readiness impact, and develop an expedited resolution plan.
 Advise the AFNWC that the item is not available through DLA and that they will need to find an alternative source. [0] Wait for the AFNWC to officially report the problem to DLA before taking any action.


3.
The position requires you to "influence, persuade, and negotiate with senior leaders." Imagine the AFNWC wants DLA to invest in a costly, specialized support solution, but DLA's initial analysis shows it's not economically viable. How would you proceed?

 Inform the AFNWC that their request is denied based on DLA's analysis.
 Present DLA's cost analysis to the AFNWC and work with them to build a business case that frames the investment in terms of mission assurance and risk mitigation, providing a more compelling justification for senior leader review.
 Agree with DLA's analysis and suggest the AFNWC de-scope their requirement to fit the existing budget.
 Escalate the disagreement directly to the DLA and AFNWC commanders to have them resolve it.


4.
This role requires the analysis of complex issues, which may involve conflicting or incomplete information. For example, imagine you receive conflicting readiness data for a critical component—the AFNWC reports low availability, while DLA's system shows ample stock. How do you resolve this discrepancy?

 I would trust the DLA system data, as it is the official system of record for inventory.
 I would advise the AFNWC that their local data must be incorrect and that they should trust the DLA numbers.
 I would treat this as a critical data integrity issue, immediately engaging with both the AFNWC unit and the DLA supply center to triangulate the data, identify the point of failure (e.g., in-transit visibility, incorrect cataloging), and establish a true operational picture.
 I would wait for the next inventory cycle to see if the numbers align on their own.


5.
As a DLA expert on the "AFNWC logistical and readiness matters." How would you ensure your knowledge remains authoritative and current?

 I would rely on the formal reports and briefings provided by the AFNWC.
 My plan is to attend the major DLA logistics conferences to stay current on DLA policies.
 I would embed myself in the AFNWC's battle rhythm by attending their planning meetings, readiness reviews, and working groups, supplementing this with continuous review of their doctrine and operational requirements.
 I would establish a good relationship with one or two key people at AFNWC who can keep me updated on important issues.


6.
This role requires incorporating DLA support into CCMD OPLANs. If you are reviewing a draft OPLAN and notice that DLA's supply chain capacity for a critical munition is overestimated, what action do you take?

 Assume the CCMD planners have data you don't and that the requirement is correct as written.
 Make a note of the discrepancy but wait until the plan is finalized to address it, as draft plans change frequently.
 Immediately contact the lead logistics planner for the OPLAN, provide them with DLA's authoritative capability data, and formally adjudicate a change to the plan's logistics annex (Annex D) to reflect a feasible support concept.
 Report the issue to DLA leadership and recommend that DLA prepare to surge capability to meet the written requirement.


7.
The "Scope and Effect" section highlights that your actions "affect the work of other experts" and can impact the "entire NC3 and nuclear weapons supply chains." Given this, if you recommend a change to a support policy, what is your process for managing its second- and third-order effects?

 My focus is on the primary goal; other experts are responsible for managing the effects on their own programs
 I would implement the change and then address any unintended consequences as they are reported.
 Before finalizing the recommendation, I would model its potential impacts across the supply chain and coordinate with other DLA and AFNWC subject matter experts to identify and mitigate downstream risks to their programs.
 I would propose the change in a general way, leaving the details of implementation and its effects for others to figure out.

A key duty is to "adjudicate...interpretive differences regarding policy" among DLA's subordinate elements. If two DLA supply centers have conflicting interpretations of a policy on supporting the AFNWC, what is your approach?

 Tell them to work it out between themselves and to only come to you if they cannot agree.
 Choose the interpretation that is most convenient for the AFNWC.
 Host a meeting with both centers, review the specific policy language in question, listen to both interpretations, and then issue a written, binding LNO decision to ensure consistent DLA-wide support to the AFNWC.
 Forward the issue to the DLA policy office to have them issue a clarification, which could take several months.

A key purpose of professional contacts in this role is to resolve non-routine problems. You discover that a new, mission-critical AFNWC component has no established DLA support plan because it's a novel technology. How do you address this "non-routine problem"?

 I would inform the AFNWC that since there is no precedent or existing policy, DLA cannot support the item at this time.

 I would suggest the AFNWC find a commercial vendor to support the component, as DLA's system is not set up for it.
