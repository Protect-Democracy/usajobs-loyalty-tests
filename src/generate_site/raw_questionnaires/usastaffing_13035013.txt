Successful completion of a full 4-year course of study in an accredited college or university leading to a bachelor's or higher degree that includes a major field of study directly related to behavioral or social science, or related disciplines appropriate to the position.

OR

OR

2. Specialized Experience - GS-09
In addition to meeting the Basic Requirement, qualified applicants must possess one year of Specialized Experience equivalent to the next lower grade (GS-07) in the Federal service, which demonstrates the ability to perform the duties of the position.

Specialized Experience is defined as 

demonstrates the ability to support intake processing, schedule assessments, assist with enrollment decisions, and document case information within a criminal-justice, social-services, or behavioral-intervention setting. Specialized experience must include:

Assisting with intake or screening activities by gathering documentation, verifying supervisee information, reviewing case records, and identifying basic eligibility or participation requirements.
Using a case-management or data-tracking system to enter, update, and maintain case notes, intake actions, appointments, and participation status.
Coordinating or scheduling assessments, orientations, program appointments, or interviews for justice-involved individuals.
Supporting enrollment or placement decisions by applying established guidelines, referral criteria, or program instructions under supervision.
Monitoring attendance, participation, or compliance; recording updates; and communicating routine information to supervision officers, program staff, or service providers.
Preparing basic case documentation, routine summaries, or reports and ensuring adherence to agency procedures, confidentiality standards, and data-entry expectations.

Note:
Experience may have been gained in criminal justice, domestic-violence services, behavioral health, case management, social services, cognitive-behavioral programming, or similar environments involving structured interventions or court-mandated conditions.

Master's degree or two years of progressively higher-level graduate education related to the position.

2. Specialized Experience - GS-11
In addition to meeting the Basic Requirement, qualified applicants must possess one year of Specialized Experience equivalent to the next lower grade (GS-09) in the Federal service, which demonstrates the ability to perform the duties of the position.

Specialized Experience is defined as the ability to independently conduct intake interviews, schedule and coordinate assessments, evaluate criminogenic risk and needs, and enroll justice-involved individuals into appropriate cognitive-behavioral interventions using Risk-Need-Responsivity (RNR) principles. Specialized experience must include:

Reviewing supervision records, behavioral health information, toxicology results, court-ordered conditions, and risk/needs assessments to determine program eligibility and appropriate intervention pathways.
Conducting structured intake interviews, gathering and verifying case information, identifying barriers to participation, and ensuring completion of required documentation and releases.
Scheduling assessments and program orientations, enrolling individuals into the correct intervention based on criminogenic needs, responsivity factors, and available capacity.
Monitoring participation, attendance, and compliance; documenting case updates in a case-management system; and coordinating case movement with Community Supervision Officers, supervisory staff, program personnel, and interdisciplinary team members.
Preparing case documentation, progress updates, and routine statistical reports; supporting intervention scheduling and capacity management; and ensuring adherence to agency policies, confidentiality standards, and data-integrity requirements.
Experience using the SMART system (Supervision &?Management Automated Record Tracking). case-management system.

Experience may have been gained in criminal justice, behavioral interventions, cognitive-behavioral programming, case management, supervision services, or other environments involving structured interventions and court-mandated participation.

Ph.D. or three years of progressively higher-level graduate education directly related to behavioral or social science, or related disciplines appropriate to the position.
