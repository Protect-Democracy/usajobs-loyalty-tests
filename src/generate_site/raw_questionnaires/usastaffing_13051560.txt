7.
A prime contractor submits a $85,000,000 sole-source proposal for a tactical vehicle program. The proposal includes a $12,500,000 non-commercial, sole-source subcontract for a highly customized armor plating subsystem. Your pricing specialists discover that the prime contractor did not perform any cost/price analysis of the subcontractor's proposal, nor did they obtain certified cost or pricing data from the subcontractor, claiming that they will negotiate the subcontract after the prime contract is awarded to the Government.
As the NH-04 Supervisory Analyst, how should you direct your team to handle this under FAR 15.404-3 (Subcontract Pricing Responsibilities) and DFARS 215.404-3?

 Accept the prime's proposal as submitted. Since the prime contractor has not yet finalized negotiations with the subcontractor, the Government should negotiate a "not-to-exceed" budget line item of $12,500,000 and include a post-award re-opener clause in the prime contract.
 Instruct your team to challenge the proposal's completeness and compliance. Advise the prime contractor that under FAR 15.404-3(b), they are statutory required to submit a completed cost/price analysis of their subcontractor's proposal at the time of submission for all subcontracts exceeding the TINA threshold ($2,000,000}. Instruct your team to recommend that the ACO/PCO decline to establish a negotiation objective for this cost element and consider the prime's proposal incomplete. Direct the prime to obtain certified cost or pricing data from the subcontractor, conduct their own rigorous cost analysis, and submit the results to the Government before joint negotiations resume.
 Recommend that the PCO bypass the prime contractor and perform a direct Government audit of the subcontractor’s proprietary manufacturing costs, releasing the prime contractor from any further pricing liability.
 Recommend that the ACO issue a Level I CAR but allow the prime contractor to include the unvalidated $12,500,000 subcontract in the negotiation baseline to avoid delaying the program schedule.


8.
A major defense aerospace contractor has submitted a $85,000,000 sole-source proposal for advanced radar-dome assemblies. The proposal includes a completed DD Form 1547 showing a proposed $3,200,000 profit markup for Facilities Capital Employed (Block 23), calculated as follows:
• Land NBV: $500,000 proposed at 0.0% profit ($0.00)
• Buildings NBV: $8,000,000 proposed at 15.0% profit ($1,200,000)
• Equipment NBV: $2,500,000 proposed at 80.0% profit ($2,000,000)
During your team's pre-award cost audit, the lead pricing analyst discovers that the contractor's formal cost proposal (specifically Form CASB-CMB) does not claim CAS 414 Facilities Capital Cost of Money and that their proposed forward pricing rate tables show a rate of profit markups under WGL to reward their capital investments.
As the NH-04 Supervisory Analyst, how should you direct your team to handle the Capital Employed profit markup under DFARS 215.404-71-4?

 Concur with the contractor. The decision to waive CAS 414 Cost of Money is a permissible administrative shortcut that saves the Government auditing overhead, and the contractor should still receive the capital employed profit markups to encourage long-term industrial modernization.
 Recommend that the PCO unilaterally award a flat 5.0% profit premium on the contractor's direct labor costs to compensate them for the waived cost of money, ensuring they are not financially penalized for their administrative choices.
 Direct your team to reject the contractor's proposed Capital Employed profit markup in its entirety. Under DFARS 215.404-71-4(c)(2), if a contractor does not propose CAS 414 cost of money, the analyst must not allocate any profit for capital employed on DD Form 1547 (Block 23 must be set to exactly). Direct your team to remove the entire $3,200,000 capital employed profit markup from the pre-negotiation profit objective. Advise the contractor that they cannot "double-dip" by seeking profit on assets for which they have failed to establish and submit a compliant CAS 414 cost-of-money baseline.
 Direct your team to accept the 15% Buildings markup, reject the 80.0% Equipment markup, reducing the capital employed profit objective to a compromise value of $1,200,000.
