Eligibilities
1.
Current Department of Army Civilian Employees Applying on Time-Limited Assignments
    
This job opportunity announcement is open to current, permanent Army employees that would occupy the position under a time-limited promotion, reassignment or change to lower grade not to exceed (NTE). You are eligible in this category if you fall into one of the following:

•  ARMY CIVILIAN EMPLOYEES SERVING ON A PERMANENT APPOINTMENT. You must submit a copy of your most recent SF-50, Notification of Personnel Action. Block 24 of your SF-50 must be a "1" or "2" AND block 34 must be a"1." Army employees can access their SF-50 at: MyBiz.
    
•  ARMY CIVILIAN EMPLOYEES SERVING ON AN EXCEPTED SERVICE VETERANS RECRUITMENT APPOINTMENT (VRA).  If you are eligible in this category, you are required to submit an SF-50 showing current Army Excepted Service VRA appointment (block 24 must be a "1" or "2" AND block 34 must be a"2.").
    
Supporting documentation: You are required to submit the documents which prove you are a current Department of the Army employee in one of the categories described above. To obtain a copy of SF-50s from employment with the Department of Defense, go to My Biz at https://compo.dcpds.cpms.osd.mil/.
    
Note: The "current Army employee" category does not include permanent Army National Guard employees in the competitive service.

Assessment 2
You will now complete a Situational Judgement Test (SJT). This assessment presents realistic job-related scenarios. For each one, select the response that best reflects how you would handle the situation. Each question has four response options. These range from the most appropriate to the least appropriate action, but the options are presented in random order. Your task is to choose the option you believe is the best course of action in a federal workplace setting. • There are no right or wrong answers. • Your responses should reflect sound judgment and professional decision-making. • This test evaluates how you approach workplace situations, not your prior experience or background. Please answer honestly and thoughtfully. Your responses will help determine your qualifications for this position.


1.
You are managing a complex medical facility design project. The original schedule specified completion of schematic design by month 6, design development by month 10, and construction documents by month 14. However, the customer has requested significant scope changes that will impact the timeline, and the design team is expressing concerns about meeting the original milestones. How do you proceed?

 Implement a phased milestone approach that allows for scope change integration at specific checkpoints, maintaining some original dates while building in flexibility for customer-requested modifications and design team capacity constraints.
 Convene a comprehensive milestone review meeting with all stakeholders to assess scope change impacts, discuss revised milestone dates that accommodate necessary changes while maintaining project integrity, and formalize updated timelines with clear deliverable expectations.
 Maintain the original milestone schedule regardless of scope changes and design team concerns, requiring the design team to meet original deadlines through additional resources or overtime work at their own expense.
 Extend all milestone dates by a standard buffer period to accommodate scope changes and design team concerns, then notify all parties of the revised schedule without detailed analysis of the specific impacts or requirements.
 Work directly with design team to develop a recovery schedule that addresses their concerns, then coordinate with the customer to explain timeline adjustments and seek approval for milestone modifications that balance scope changes with schedule requirements.


2.
You are coordinating a major training facility project that requires integration of structural, mechanical, electrical, and environmental engineering disciplines. The planning phase has identified potential conflicts between the proposed building design and new environmental regulations, while cost engineering indicates the current design approach may exceed budget constraints. How do you coordinate the various engineering disciplines to address these challenges?

 Schedule sequential meetings with each engineering discipline to understand their specific concerns and requirements, then develop a coordination plan that addresses environmental and cost issues through design modifications and alternative approaches identified by the technical teams.
 Direct each engineering discipline to independently develop solutions for their area of responsibility, then compile their recommendations into a comprehensive project plan that addresses environmental and cost concerns through separate work packages.
 Establish an integrated design team with representatives from each engineering discipline, facilitate collaborative sessions to identify design solutions that meet environmental requirements within budget constraints, and develop a coordinated project plan that sequences work to optimize interdisciplinary coordination and minimize rework.
 Prioritize the original design approach and direct engineering disciplines to find ways to meet environmental regulations and budget constraints without modifying the fundamental project concept or coordination approach.
 Create a matrix organization structure that assigns lead responsibility for environmental and cost issues to specific disciplines, while maintaining regular coordination meetings to ensure all engineering considerations are integrated into the overall project plan.


3.
During the design phase of a federal facility, the customer expresses concerns about the project's progress and claims they were not adequately informed about recent technical issues that may impact the schedule and budget. Other stakeholders, including the Project Delivery Team and other internal technical teams, have different perspectives on the adequacy of communication. How do you reconcile these concerns?

 Conduct individual meetings with each stakeholder to understand their perspectives, facilitate a comprehensive project review meeting to clarify technical issues and their impacts, establish improved communication protocols, and implement regular updates to prevent future misunderstandings.
 Meet with the customer to understand their specific concerns and information needs, then coordinate with other stakeholders to develop a communication plan that addresses identified gaps while maintaining appropriate information flow.
 Acknowledge the customer's concerns and provide them with detailed project status information, then remind all stakeholders of their communication responsibilities and increase the frequency of status reports.
 Organize a stakeholder meeting to address the customer's concerns directly, review the technical issues and their actual impacts, and establish enhanced communication procedures to ensure all parties receive timely and accurate project information.
 Explain to the customer that technical issues are normal in complex projects and that the current communication approach is standard practice, while continuing existing communication procedures without significant modifications.

The Using Service emphasizes the importance of these changes for mission readiness and requests immediate action before the next milestone review. What is your approach?

 Inform the Using Service that the requested changes cannot be accommodated within the current design and proceed with the original layout without further coordination.

 Direct the design team to incorporate the requested changes immediately and prepare updated cost estimates to address potential funding impacts later.
 Modify the design to include the requested changes while reducing other essential components to stay within the programmed scope, without notifying the PM or documenting the adjustments.

 Convene a multi-stakeholder coordination meeting to review the new requirements, assess their impact on the design, schedule, and budget, and develop a revised project plan that incorporates compliance strategies while addressing customer concerns. Document the agreed-upon changes and communicate them to all stakeholders.

6.
As Project Design Manager, you observe that multiple disciplines have rushed drawings to meet the 65% design milestone for a federal medical facility. Mechanical and structural coordination remains unresolved, and fire protection calculations are incomplete. Section chiefs argue that "we can fix it at 95%," but you know quality deficiencies at this stage often cascade into rework, schedule slip, and increased costs. How do you proceed?

 Reduce QA/QC requirements to accelerate milestone completion and avoid delays, assuming that minor deficiencies will not significantly impact the project.
 Require completion of critical coordination and life-safety elements before accepting the milestone, adjust the review schedule if necessary to preserve design integrity, and communicate the importance of quality to all stakeholders.
 Accept the milestone as-is to protect performance metrics and rely on later phases to resolve issues, while reminding section chiefs of the importance of addressing deficiencies.
 Accept the submittal but formally document deficiencies, assign corrective actions with deadlines, and monitor closely to ensure issues are resolved before the next milestone.
 Approve the package and depend on Construction review to identify remaining issues during the build phase, assuming they can be resolved without major impacts.

 Collaborate with the budget analyst to conduct comprehensive cost analysis of current and projected expenses, develop multiple funding scenarios with associated scope and schedule implications, identify potential contingency fund sources, and present options to leadership with recommendations for maintaining project viability.
 Direct the budget analyst to reallocate funds from other district projects to cover the shortfall, and continue the project as planned without scope or schedule modifications since the work is already underway.
 Request that the budget analyst identify additional funding sources to cover projected shortfalls, while continuing current project activities and deferring major decisions until funding availability is clarified.
