An incarcerated individual with a history of polysubstance abuse has been referred to you after exhibiting unpredictable behavior and making statements that suggest possible cognitive impairment. The custody team reports the individual has difficulty following instructions and shows marked personality changes. You need to develop a comprehensive psychological profile to inform treatment planning and determine the individual's capacity to participate in the residential drug abuse program.


Which action represents the most effective and appropriate psychological assessment approach in this situation?
 Conduct a clinical interview and administer multiple assessment instruments including an objective personality measure (such as MMPI-2) and a neuropsychological screening instrument to assess cognitive functioning, then integrate all available information including custody reports to develop a comprehensive diagnostic formulation and treatment recommendations.
 Under the guidance of the facility's Chief Psychologist, administer two different types of assessments including a projective personality measure and an intelligence assessment, score the tests, interpret the results with supervision, and document findings in the individual's treatment file.
 Observe a senior licensed psychologist conduct similar psychological assessments, assist by scoring the neuropsychological screening instruments and objective personality measures according to established testing protocols, and review how the psychologist integrates multiple sources of information to formulate diagnostic impressions.
 With supervision from a licensed psychologist, conduct a structured clinical interview and administer a diagnostic checklist for substance use disorders, participate in scoring the assessment instruments, and assist in interpreting the results while discussing the case formulation with your supervisor.
 None of the above.


2.
An incarcerated individual in your substance abuse treatment program has been diagnosed with co-occurring opioid use disorder and major depressive disorder with trauma history related to childhood abuse. During individual sessions, the individual reports intrusive memories, demonstrates negative thought patterns about self-worth, and expresses hopelessness about recovery. The treatment team is meeting to develop an updated treatment plan and is looking to you for specific intervention recommendations that address both the substance use and mental health concerns.

Which action represents the most effective and appropriate treatment intervention recommendation approach in this situation?
 Review the individual's case file thoroughly, observe recordings of previous clinical interviews, and recommend implementing cognitive-behavioral interventions to address the negative thought patterns and behavioral approaches with contingency management to support substance use recovery based on your independent clinical judgment as a psychologist.
 Schedule a consultation with your supervising psychologist to discuss the case, then recommend a treatment plan incorporating cognitive-behavioral therapy for the depression and trauma-focused interventions such as Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR) for the trauma symptoms, while continuing medication-assisted treatment for the opioid use disorder, documenting your supervisor's guidance in the treatment plan.
 Review current research literature on evidence-based treatments for co-occurring disorders, discuss potential treatment recommendations with supervisors based on knowledge of cognitive-behavioral interventions and relaxation training techniques, and study case vignettes demonstrating similar presentations to understand appropriate treatment approaches for this clinical presentation.
 While working under the supervision of a licensed psychologist, conduct therapy sessions using cognitive interventions and behavioral techniques, make treatment recommendations based on your knowledge of at least two different treatment modalities including family systems approaches and impulse control strategies, and document your recommendations with supervisor review.

3.
You are leading a weekly cognitive-behavioral therapy group in the Residential Drug Abuse Program with eight incarcerated individuals at various stages of recovery. During today's session focused on relapse prevention, one participant discloses experiencing intense cravings and identifies distorted thinking patterns that previously led to substance use ("I'll never be able to stay clean, so why bother trying"). Other group members begin sharing similar experiences. You recognize this as a critical teaching moment to address cognitive distortions and provide therapeutic interventions that will benefit all group members.

Which action represents the most effective and appropriate psychotherapy approach in this situation?
 Facilitate the group therapy session by implementing cognitive-behavioral techniques to help participants identify and challenge distorted thoughts, guide them through cognitive restructuring exercises, teach alternative coping strategies for managing cravings, and process the experience with all group members to reinforce relapse prevention skills.
 Co-facilitate the session with a senior licensed psychologist who provides direction as you work with the group to address the cognitive distortions, implement behavioral interventions for craving management with your supervisor's guidance, and process the session afterward with your supervisor to improve your therapeutic skills.
 Assist the licensed psychologist leading the group by reinforcing psycho-educational materials about relapse prevention and cognitive distortions, co-facilitate portions of the group session where you help participants practice identifying distorted thoughts, and observe how the licensed psychologist therapeutically addresses the disclosures to learn advanced group facilitation techniques.
 Under the supervision of a licensed psychologist, conduct the psychotherapy group using cognitive-behavioral therapy techniques to address the distorted thinking, implement relaxation training to help manage the cravings, and meet with your supervisor after the session to discuss the interventions you used and receive feedback on your therapeutic approach.
 None of the above.


4.
During a regular afternoon substance abuse treatment group session, one of the participants suddenly becomes agitated and begins hyperventilating while disclosing a traumatic memory. The individual stands up abruptly, appears disoriented, and states "I can't breathe, I think I'm dying." Other group members show visible concern and the tension in the room is escalating rapidly. As the Drug Abuse Program Coordinator, you need to manage this crisis situation immediately while ensuring the safety and therapeutic environment for all participants.

Which action represents the most effective and appropriate crisis response approach in this situation?
 Immediately assess the situation for safety concerns, use a calm and authoritative voice to stabilize the environment, implement grounding techniques with the distressed individual while maintaining awareness of the group, direct another staff member to contact medical if symptoms persist, and once the immediate crisis is de-escalated, process the event therapeutically with the entire group to prevent ongoing disruption.
 Recognize the signs of a panic attack and use your clinical skills to help the individual regulate their breathing through guided techniques, remain calm to model appropriate crisis response for the group, address the other group members' concerns to prevent the situation from escalating, and once the individual is stabilized, continue the session while arranging for a follow-up individual session to address the trigger.
 React appropriately to the medical emergency by immediately calling for institutional medical assistance through the emergency response system, ensure other group members remain calm and seated, stay with the distressed individual offering reassurance, and after medical staff arrive and assess the situation, document the incident thoroughly in the individual's treatment file.
 Use your clinical skills to manage the disruptive situation by implementing de-escalation techniques, guide the distressed individual through breathing exercises to reduce the hyperventilation, provide education to the other group members about panic responses to traumatic memories, and refer the individual to the institution's crisis intervention team for follow-up assessment.
 None of the above.

You have provided informal feedback twice, but the performance issues continue. Meanwhile, one of your Psychology Technicians has expressed interest in advancing their career and taking on more clinical responsibilities. Your annual performance evaluations are due next month.

Which action represents the most effective and appropriate supervisory approach in this situation?
 Schedule a formal counseling session with the Treatment Specialist to document specific performance deficiencies and expectations for improvement, complete a performance evaluation that clearly addresses both individual strengths and areas needing improvement, establish a timeline for corrective action before considering performance-based disciplinary measures, meet with the Psychology Technician to discuss career development goals and identify training opportunities, and adjust staff duties by assigning the Psychology Technician to shadow senior staff on more complex clinical cases while redistributing some of the Treatment Specialist's responsibilities until performance improves.
 Provide regular feedback to both employees about their on-going work, hold the Treatment Specialist accountable for completing daily assignments by implementing a tracking system and monitoring closely, work with the Psychology Technician as a career development advisor by reviewing their professional goals and discussing advancement opportunities, train the Psychology Technician on advanced procedures to support their development, and set clear priorities and goals for both staff members to accomplish the program's objectives.
 Develop improved operational procedures for the program that include clearer documentation standards and attendance expectations that will benefit all staff, organize and present specialized training on professional engagement and counseling skills for the entire team, represent the Drug Abuse Program at the department head meeting to discuss programmatic needs, and create a positive example through your leadership by modeling punctuality and thorough documentation practices.
 Hold the Treatment Specialist accountable by providing direct feedback about the missed deadlines and late arrivals, train the Psychology Technician on department procedures for handling more advanced clinical responsibilities, assist in developing specialized training on documentation requirements that both employees can attend, and set clear priorities for your unit to accomplish program objectives while ensuring adequate coverage of all group sessions.
