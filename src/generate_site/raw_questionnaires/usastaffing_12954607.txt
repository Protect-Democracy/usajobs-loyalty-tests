 Implement a new policy requiring all project managers to submit their material requests six months in advance to improve forecasting.

 Make a business case to senior management for a budget increase, arguing that the current budget is insufficient to meet operational demands.

An audit reveals a significant discrepancy between the physical inventory count and the records in your inventory management system. This is the third time this has happened in a year. What is the most effective first step to solving this problem?
 Implement a daily cycle counting program to catch discrepancies faster.
 Bring in a consultant to evaluate and recommend a new inventory management software system.
 Hold an all-hands meeting with the inventory team to stress the importance of accuracy and review existing procedures.
 Initiate a root cause analysis by reviewing cycle count procedures, system transaction logs, and staff workflows to identify where the process is failing.

Your team uses a specific software application for inventory control that is now outdated and unsupported, leading to frequent system crashes and data loss. The cost to upgrade is significant. What is the best problem-solving approach to present to senior management?
 Present a side-by-side feature comparison of the old software and three new potential systems.
 Document the full cost of the upgrade and request that it be made the top priority for any year-end discretionary funds.
 A business case that quantifies the financial impact of the system failures (e.g., costs from project delays, labor hours spent on data recovery) and compares the cost of upgrading versus the cost of continued operational risk.
 Ask for a modest budget to hire a temporary data entry clerk to manually back up the system daily and mitigate the risk of data loss.

 Provide the customer with the supplier's contact information so they can get a status update directly from the source.
 Assure the customer that you have escalated the issue internally and that they will receive an update as soon as one is available.

Senior management has announced a new long-term organizational goal to significantly reduce its carbon footprint and increase the use of sustainable materials. How should you, as the Supervisory Supply Systems Analyst, respond to this new strategic direction?
 Begin researching and vetting new suppliers who specialize in sustainable materials, analyze the long-term cost-benefit of switching, and propose a phased implementation plan for your department.

12.

 Formally document the trend in your records in case an issue arises later, but take no immediate action since operations are currently unaffected.
 Begin building up a small "safety stock" of the materials, using the existing budget, as a temporary buffer against future price increases.
 Prepare a briefing for leadership that outlines the trend, identifies the potential risks to the organization's mission, and recommends proactive strategies like qualifying alternative materials or diversifying the supplier base.
 Open a dialogue with your primary supplier for those materials to ask about their plans for mitigating volatility and request preferential pricing.
