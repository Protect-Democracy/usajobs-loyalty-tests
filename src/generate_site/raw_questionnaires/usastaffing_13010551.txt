2.
You oversee a Business Office within the DLA enterprise that must build up staffing and resourcing to support an expanded mission. The office supports mission-critical administrative, financial, manpower, requirements development, and business-operations functions. How should you assess the situation and build sustainable capability?

 Fill critical vacancies first and use the new staff’s observations, along with existing workload data, to identify functions requiring the most immediate management attention and additional resources.
 Identify mission-critical work, workforce and skill gaps, workload demand, control risks, and resource constraints; prioritize gaps, set recovery measures, and implement a phased staffing and process-improvement plan.
 Survey employees, supervisors, and key customers regarding workload concerns, process delays, and resource shortfalls; use the feedback to redistribute tasks, revise priorities, and support resource requests.
 Maintain current processes and priorities while documenting workload challenges, monitoring mission impacts, and awaiting additional staffing, funding, or higher-level direction before making significant changes.


3.
Your two GS-14 Division Chiefs (one leading the Business Office and the other leading Acquisition Requirements) are locked in a dispute. The Chief of Acquisition Requirements argues that staff shortages in their division are jeopardizing National Defense Stockpile contract awards and demands that two Management Analysts be reassigned from the Business Office Division. The Chief of the Business Office strongly objects, stating that their analysts are critical to an ongoing financial audit. This internal conflict is affecting morale and delaying operations. How do you resolve this dispute?

 Convene both Chiefs to review workload timelines, temporarily assign the analysts to high-priority acquisitions part-time, and develop a long-term hiring strategy to resolve the structural staffing gaps.
 Meet with both Chiefs, hear their arguments, and create a unilateral, binding decision to split the difference—temporarily reassigning one analyst to Acquisitions while retaining the other in Admin to ensure both programs can continue with minimal disruption.
 It is important to delegate tasks. Inform both Chiefs that as senior GS-14 leaders, they are expected to resolve operational staffing issues collaboratively. Instruct them to return to your office in 48 hours with a mutually agreed-upon solution.
 Side with the Acquisitions Division Chief due to the immediate, high-priority nature of stockpile contract awards, and order the immediate transfer of both analysts, telling the Admin Chief they will have to find alternate ways to support the audit.
 Instruct both Division Chiefs to freeze all personnel actions and submit a formal request to the J7 SM Director for additional GS-13 positions to avoid having to make difficult resource-allocation decisions. Personnel decisions should be vetted at the highest level of approval.


4.
A mid-year budget review indicates that a critical National Defense Stockpile (NDS) budget program that you manage will have an unexpected 15% funding shortfall by the end of the fiscal year due to increasing mission requirements, risking nationwide operational readiness. As a DLA Fund Holder and Authorizing Official, what is your most appropriate course of action?

 Initiate an immediate, rigorous portfolio-wide review within the Directorate to identify low-priority offsets. These low-priority offsets may be from budget lines unrelated to the priority program. Draft a formal reprogramming request to shift available unobligated funds from one budget line to the other.
 Direct the GS-14 Division Chiefs to execute an immediate, flat 15% budget reduction across all active Directorate programs to resolve the deficit quickly, without initiating formal inter-directorate reprogramming actions.
 Promptly request supplementary emergency Defense Working Capital Funding from DLA, while simultaneously instructing your GS-14 Division Chiefs to place a temporary freeze on non-essential operational costs (such as travel and administrative contracts) to mitigate the current deficit.

How do you manage these efforts?
