5.
You are the Lead Transportation Assistant overseeing the day's shipments. You observe a carrier's driver attempting to load a pallet of hazardous materials (HAZMAT), but you notice the vehicle has not been properly placarded for the material as required by the Defense Transportation Regulation (DTR). What is your immediate and most appropriate course of action?

 Allow the shipment to proceed to avoid delaying a critical delivery but make a note to report the carrier for non-compliance later.
 Instruct the driver to wait, find the correct placards in base supply, and affix them to the vehicle yourself to get the shipment out quickly.
 Immediately halt the loading process, inform the driver that the vehicle is not in compliance with DTR Part II (Cargo Movement) placarding requirements, and state that the shipment cannot proceed until the carrier corrects the deficiency. Document the incident as per local procedure.
 Let the driver finish loading but refuse to sign the shipping documents until they call their dispatch and get approval to move without the placards.


6.
You are arranging a monthly recurring shipment. The incumbent Transportation Service Provider (TSP) has performed adequately. However, while preparing the shipment in the Global Freight Management (GFM) system, you see a newly approved TSP can move the cargo for a 15% lower rate. According to the principle of 'Best Value' in transportation, what key factors must you consider in addition to cost before selecting the new, cheaper carrier?

 The only factor is cost. The lowest-cost carrier that meets the required delivery date must be selected to ensure fiscal responsibility.
 You must evaluate other performance and risk factors, such as the new TSP's safety record, on-time delivery percentage, and any historical data on cargo loss or damage before making a selection.
 You should continue using the incumbent carrier because they are a known entity. Switching to a new provider, even a cheaper one, introduces unnecessary risk to the supply chain.
 Contact the new TSP and ask for a written guarantee that they will deliver the items faster than the incumbent carrier to justify the switch.


7.
A shipment of critical repair parts arrives at your receiving dock. During the offload, you and the carrier's driver notice that one of the crates is visibly crushed on the corner. The driver is waiting for you to sign the delivery receipt. What is the correct procedure to follow when documenting a transportation discrepancy of this nature?

 Immediately sign for the delivery to release the driver and then begin the process of inspecting the freight and filing a damage report.
 Note the specific damage in writing on the carrier's delivery receipt before signing it. Accept the freight and then segregate the damaged crate and initiate a Transportation Discrepancy Report (TDR).
 Refuse the entire shipment, including the undamaged crates, and instruct the driver to return it to the point of origin due to the observed damage.
 Tell the driver to set the damaged crate aside and take the rest. Advise them you cannot sign for any of it until your supervisor personally inspects the damage.
