3.
A Defense Contract Management Agency (DCMA) Administrative Contracting Officer (ACO) administering a contract advises the First-Line Supervisor that a critical contractor payment is delinquent. The ACO has not yet determined whether the problem involves the contract funding record, an Accounting Classification Reference Number (ACRN), invoice or receiving-report data, a disbursement record, or another administrative error. To resolve the delinquency quickly, the ACO proposes issuing a unilateral modification to move funds from another contract line item. What is the most appropriate supervisory guidance?
 Advise the ACO to contact the Procuring Contracting Officer (PCO) and the Defense Finance and Accounting Service (DFAS) immediately and request a funding correction before performing a reconciliation because the delinquency itself establishes that the contract funding is incorrect.
 Advise the ACO to issue the unilateral modification immediately to avoid further payment delay and reconcile the records after the contractor is paid.
 Direct the ACO to immediately initiate reconciliation of the applicable contract funding and payment records, including review at the appropriate ACRN and contract-line-item level, to identify the source of the discrepancy. Coordinate concurrently with the responsible organization, such as the PCO, funding activity, or DFAS payment office, as the facts require. Do not move funds merely to resolve an unexplained payment problem; any modification or funding action must be within the ACO's authority, supported by the reconciliation, and legally permissible.
 Advise the ACO to wait for the contractor to submit a claim before researching the payment discrepancy because a formal dispute is required before DCMA can act.


4.
A physically complete cost-reimbursement contract assigned to DCMA for contract administration is otherwise ready for closeout. Final direct costs and indirect rates for two performance years have not been settled, and final rate settlement is expected to take approximately 18 months. The ACO asks the First-Line Supervisor for guidance on whether quick-closeout procedures should be considered. What is the most appropriate supervisory guidance?
 Advise the ACO to keep the contract open until final direct costs and indirect rates are established because cost-reimbursement contracts cannot be closed before final rate settlement.
 Direct the ACO to evaluate and document whether quick closeout is appropriate under Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) 42.708 and Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement (DFARS) 242.708. Determine whether the unsettled direct and indirect costs are relatively insignificant, perform and document the required risk assessment, including consideration of relevant cognizant auditor concerns and the other FAR 42.708 risk factors, and determine whether agreement can be reached with the contractor on a reasonable estimate of allocable direct and indirect costs. If those conditions are satisfied, the ACO shall negotiate a quick-closeout settlement and close the contract before final direct-cost and indirect-rate determinations.
 Advise the ACO to use the contractor's current provisional billing rates as final rates and close the contract because they are the best available estimate.
 Advise the ACO to deobligate the remaining funds and administratively close the contract, then resolve remaining cost differences after closeout.


5.
A DCMA ACO asks the First-Line Supervisor for advice after a program office requests that DCMA direct a substantially different technical approach that would roughly double the contract effort and add work not contemplated at award. The program office wants the work directed under the Changes clause to avoid acquisition delay. The contract is assigned to DCMA for administration, but the ACO must act within the scope of assigned contract-administration authority. What is the most appropriate supervisory guidance?
 Advise the ACO to issue a unilateral change order because the Changes clause permits the Government to change technical requirements, with price addressed later through an equitable adjustment.
 Advise the ACO to process the requirement as an administrative modification because the program office initiated the change.
 Advise the ACO to direct the contractor orally and document the direction after performance begins because urgency permits work outside the contract's general scope.
 Advise the ACO not to direct the work under the Changes clause unless the proposed change is determined to be within the contract's general scope, within an area authorized by the applicable Changes clause, and within the ACO's delegated authority. If the requirement is outside the contract's general scope, or if the ACO lacks authority to issue or negotiate the change, refer the requirement to the buying activity for an appropriate acquisition action.


6.
An ACO asks the First-Line Supervisor for guidance during closeout of a physically complete contract identified as firm-fixed-price that has an Unliquidated Obligation (ULO) balance. The ACO believes the balance is excess and proposes immediately deobligating the funds. What is the most appropriate supervisory guidance?
 Advise the ACO to immediately deobligate the entire ULO because the contract is identified as firm-fixed-price and physically complete; therefore, any ULO must be excess.
 Direct the ACO to conduct and document the required funds reconciliation before deobligating the ULO. Review the actual contract, Contract Line Item Number (CLIN) structure, and applicable clauses; verify whether the contract is entirely firm-fixed-price or contains both FFP and flexibly priced line items (a hybrid contract); confirm that the contract type and applicable CLINs are correctly reflected in the Mechanization of Contract Administration Services (MOCAS) system; and determine the reason for the ULO. Based on that review, determine whether the ULO represents excess or remaining funds and take the applicable closeout action.
 Advise the ACO to retain the entire ULO until contract closeout because funds cannot be deobligated from a physically complete contract until the closeout process is complete.
 Advise the ACO to treat the ULO as remaining funds and Q-Final the contract because physical completion establishes that no additional contractor payment is due.


7.
A DCMA ACO receives a Defense Contract Audit Agency (DCAA) incurred-cost audit report issued after March 22, 2023, identifying questioned direct costs on a cost-reimbursement contract. The PCO has delegated responsibility to resolve the questioned direct costs after prior coordination with the ACO and agreement by the CMO Contracts Director. The accepted delegation is documented in MOCAS, and the PCO's signed delegation letter is in the official contract file. The ACO asks the First-Line Supervisor what must occur before negotiating or otherwise resolving the questioned direct costs with the contractor. What is the most appropriate supervisory guidance?

 Direct the ACO to begin negotiations immediately using the DCAA-questioned-cost amount as the Government's position and prepare a Price Negotiation Memorandum (PNM) only after agreement is reached.
 Direct the ACO to return the audit report to DCAA because only the auditor may negotiate and settle questioned direct costs with the contractor.
 Direct the ACO to evaluate the DCAA findings, the contractor's responses and supporting records, applicable contract terms, FAR Part 31, and other relevant information; coordinate with DCAA as needed to clarify audit findings or obtain additional audit input; and prepare the appropriate pre-disposition documentation—a Memorandum for Record (MFR) or Pre-Negotiation Objective Memorandum (PNOM), as applicable—that states the Government's position and affirmatively agrees or disagrees with each relevant DCAA finding and recommendation. The ACO must provide sound rationale, obtain required management review and any applicable Board of Review review before negotiating a settlement, withdrawing a notice, or issuing a contracting officer's final decision.
 Direct the ACO to disallow all questioned direct costs without considering contractor evidence, documenting the ACO's position, obtaining management review, or confirming that the ACO holds delegated authority.


8.
A PCO requests DCMA input on a proposed contract-type recommendation for a development effort with significant technical and cost uncertainty. The Government can establish a target cost and seeks a cost-reimbursement arrangement that motivates cost control by adjusting fee based on the relationship between target cost and actual allowable cost. The assigned DCMA ACO asks the First-Line Supervisor for guidance on the recommendation DCMA should provide. Which recommendation should the supervisor endorse?

 Recommend a Cost-Plus-Incentive-Fee (CPIF) contract, using a target cost, target fee, minimum and maximum fees, and a fee-adjustment formula tied to the relationship between total target cost and total allowable cost.
 Recommend a Fixed-Price-Incentive, Firm Target (FPIF) contract because significant cost uncertainty always requires the contractor to bear costs above a ceiling.
 Recommend a Cost-Plus-Fixed-Fee (CPFF) contract because its fixed fee automatically increases when the contractor underruns estimated cost.
 Recommend a Firm-Fixed-Price (FFP) contract because FFP is required whenever the Government wants a strong cost-control incentive, regardless of cost uncertainty.


9.
A DCMA ACO receives a PCO request for analytical support concerning a contractor's assertion that a product modified for military use remains a commercial product. The PCO has not requested that a DCMA Commercial Item Group (CIG) contracting officer make the commercial-product determination. The ACO asks the First-Line Supervisor for guidance on the appropriate analysis and use of DCMA resources. What is the most appropriate supervisory guidance?

 Accept the assertion if the contractor expects substantial future sales to the general public, even if the modified product has not otherwise met the FAR definition.
 Accept the assertion when the contractor prefers commercial terms because contractor business practices are the primary basis for commerciality.
 Advise the ACO to coordinate with the cognizant DCMA CIG and appropriate technical personnel, as needed, to evaluate the item against the FAR 2.101 commercial-product definition and supporting market and technical information. Develop and provide analysis supporting the PCO's determination whether the underlying product meets a FAR 2.101 commercial-product criterion and whether the modification is either of a type customarily available in the commercial marketplace or, if made to meet Federal Government requirements and not customarily available commercially, is a minor modification that does not significantly alter the nongovernmental function or essential physical characteristics of the item or component, or change the purpose of a process.
 Treat the item as commercial whenever its unit price is below the simplified acquisition threshold.


10.
An ACO asks the First-Line Supervisor for guidance on an incrementally funded cost-reimbursement contract containing FAR 52.232-22, Limitation of Funds. The contractor has provided written notification that it expects its costs to reach the amount currently allotted to the contract and additional funds will be required to continue performance. What is the most appropriate supervisory guidance?

 Direct the ACO to issue a modification adding the additional funds because administration of the contract has been delegated to DCMA and the contractor has provided the required Limitation of Funds notice.
 Direct the ACO to verify the contractor's notification and funding status, notify the PCO of the amount of additional funds required and the contractor's notification status, and follow the Government funding decision. The PCO determines whether additional funds are available and, if approved, issues the modification obligating additional funds. The ACO should subsequently verify that the funds are correctly obligated in MOCAS. The ACO should not direct continued performance beyond the allotted amount based solely on the contractor's notice.
 Advise the contractor to continue performance while the ACO requests additional funds because timely notification under FAR 52.232-22 obligates the Government to fund the additional costs.
 Advise the ACO to instruct the contractor to submit its final voucher immediately because reaching the allotted funding amount automatically requires contract closeout.


11.
A DCMA Administrative Contracting Officer (ACO), acting within delegated authority for a sole-source contract modification, asks the First-Line Supervisor for guidance. The contractor's proposed indirect rates materially exceed historical actual rates. The proposal and available contract-administration records do not adequately explain the variance or establish whether the proposed rates are reasonable. What is the most appropriate supervisory guidance?

 Direct the ACO to request a full DCAA audit immediately because proposed indirect rates that exceed historical actual rates are presumptively unreasonable.
 Direct the ACO to establish the negotiation objective using historical actual rates because a contractor may not propose indirect rates above prior-year actual experience.
 Advise the ACO to perform and document a cost analysis of the proposed indirect rates, considering available contractor data, historical actuals, current forecasts, applicable Forward Pricing Rate Agreement / Forward Pricing Rate Recommendation (FPRA/FPRR) or other forward-pricing information, prior DCMA and DCAA results, and relevant changes to indirect-cost pools and allocation bases. Determine whether the Contract Administration Office (CAO) is a designated cost-monitoring site and use Cost Monitoring Specialist (CMS) support when applicable; in the absence of a CMS, ensure the applicable CMS functions are performed. Coordinate with the cognizant DCMA Pricing Team and obtain technical-pricing or other functional support, as needed, and coordinate with DCAA when additional, nonduplicative audit support is warranted.
 Direct the ACO to accept the proposed rates to support timely definitization because final indirect-rate settlement will resolve any uncertainty in the rates.


12.
A DCMA ACO receives an advisory audit identifying potential defective certified cost or pricing data on a negotiated contract action. Certified cost or pricing data were required and obtained. The audit indicates that data reasonably available to the contractor before the applicable 'as of' date was not disclosed or were inaccurate, incomplete, or noncurrent, and that the data may have increased the negotiated price. The PCO has formally delegated the defective-pricing case to DCMA, DCMA has accepted the delegation, and the ACO is authorized to perform the applicable FAR 15.407-1 defective-pricing functions for the action. The ACO asks the First-Line Supervisor for guidance on disposition of the audit findings. What is the most appropriate supervisory guidance?

 Direct the ACO to reduce the contract price by the entire amount questioned by DCAA because an advisory audit finding establishes both defective pricing and the amount of the Government's entitlement.
 Direct the ACO to return the audit to DCAA and request that DCAA make the final defective-pricing determination because the auditor, rather than the contracting officer, determines whether a price adjustment is required.
 Advise the ACO that no postaward action is available because certified cost or pricing data are relevant only to negotiations conducted before award.
 Advise the ACO to evaluate and document the advisory audit findings and other relevant evidence; coordinate with DCAA regarding the audit findings and maintain appropriate coordination with the PCO throughout disposition of the delegated case; give the contractor an opportunity to support the accuracy, completeness, and currency of the questioned data; and determine whether the certified cost or pricing data were defective and whether the defect resulted in a significant increase in the negotiated contract price. In making that assessment, the ACO should consider the rebuttable presumption of Government reliance recognized in defective-pricing case law, together with any evidence that rebuts that presumption. The ACO should consider allowable offsets and other required factors, document the determination and corrective action, and take the appropriate disposition action within the scope of the accepted delegation. If a price reduction is warranted, the ACO should execute the appropriate modification in coordination with the PCO and pursue recovery of any overpayment, including applicable interest on the overpayment, and any potential penalty in accordance with the applicable clause and FAR/DFARS requirements.


13.
A DCMA ACO receives a request from a PCO for DCMA field-pricing support for a competitive cost-reimbursement source selection. One offeror proposes labor hours and rates materially below the Government estimate and below what the Government technical evaluators believe is achievable under that offeror's proposed technical approach. The ACO asks the First-Line Supervisor for guidance on DCMA's cost-analysis support. What is the most appropriate supervisory guidance?

 Advise the ACO to use the proposed cost without adjustment and address the unrealistically low estimate only as a technical or proposal-risk weakness.
 Advise the ACO to coordinate with the cognizant DCMA Pricing Team and appropriate technical personnel to evaluate the realism of the offeror's proposed labor hours, labor rates, labor mix, indirect rates, and other relevant cost elements in relation to its specific technical approach. The analysis should identify unrealistic proposed-cost elements, develop a supported analysis of probable cost reflecting the Government's best estimate of the cost most likely to result from the offeror's proposal, and provide the resulting analysis and recommendation to the PCO for use in the source-selection evaluation consistent with the solicitation's evaluation criteria.
 Advise the ACO to recommend rejection of the proposal as nonresponsive because the proposed cost is below the Government estimate.
 Advise the ACO to recommend acceptance of the proposed cost without adjustment because a lower proposed cost is necessarily more advantageous to the Government.


14.
A DCMA ACO receives a PCO request for field-pricing support for a sole-source commercial-product procurement. Adequate price competition is unavailable, and certified cost or pricing data are not required because the commercial-product exception applies. The contractor's proposed price is not supported by competition. The ACO asks the First-Line Supervisor for guidance on the analytical approach. When adequate data are available, which approach should the supervisor favor?

 Rely primarily on an independent Government estimate even when reliable comparable prior-sales data are available.
 Rely on a published catalog or list price without determining whether it provides meaningful evidence of actual market pricing or sales.
 Accept the proposed price because a commercial-product determination itself establishes that the price is fair and reasonable.
 Advise the ACO to coordinate with the cognizant DCMA Pricing Team and obtain technical-pricing support, as needed, to use price analysis based on actual prices at which the same or similar items have previously been sold to Government or non-Government customers. Determine whether the prior sales are a valid basis for comparison and adjust for material differences in quantity, terms and conditions, configuration, market or economic factors, and escalation. If the available sales data are insufficient to determine that the proposed price is fair and reasonable, obtain and evaluate appropriate data other than certified cost or pricing data.
