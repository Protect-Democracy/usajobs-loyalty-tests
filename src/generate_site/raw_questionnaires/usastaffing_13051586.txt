A residency program involves training in a specialized field of medicine in a hospital or an institution accredited for training in the specialty by a recognized body of the American Medical Association(external link) (AMA), American Osteopathic Association (AOA) or Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education (ACGME)(external link).

A fellowship program involves advanced training (beyond residency training) in a given medical specialty in either a clinical or research setting in a hospital or an institution accredited in the United States for such training.

A 45-year-old inmate presents to the medical unit with acute chest pain that began 30 minutes ago while exercising in the recreation yard. He describes the pain as crushing, radiating to his left arm and jaw, accompanied by shortness of breath and diaphoresis. His medical history includes smoking, hypertension, and a family history of early cardiac disease. Vital signs show blood pressure 160/95, heart rate 110, respiratory rate 22, and oxygen saturation 94% on room air.

Which action represents the most effective and appropriate clinical assessment and treatment approach in this situation?


 Perform a comprehensive history and physical examination appropriate to the chief complaint; develop a logical differential diagnosis including acute coronary syndrome; immediately order and independently interpret an electrocardiogram (EKG) to identify acute findings; perform Advanced Cardiac Life Support as certified if needed; order appropriate cardiac biomarkers and chest x-ray in logical sequence; independently determine initial management including oxygen, aspirin, and nitroglycerin; arrange immediate transfer to an outside facility with cardiac catheterization capabilities; and provide medical consultation to staff regarding this complex acute presentation.
 Take a detailed patient history and conduct a thorough physical examination; formulate a differential diagnosis that includes cardiac and non-cardiac causes; order preliminary diagnostic tests including EKG, cardiac enzymes, and chest x-ray; review the test results and identify common acute conditions; consult cardiology interpretation for the EKG to confirm preliminary impressions; perform CPR and Advanced Cardiac Life Support as certified if the patient's condition deteriorates; and coordinate transfer to an outside hospital for definitive cardiac care.
 Admit the patient for observation and monitoring; consult with a cardiologist before ordering diagnostic tests to ensure the appropriate workup is performed; provide supportive care including oxygen and pain management; turn over the patient's care to the emergency department physician at the receiving hospital; document the presentation and transfer in the medical record; and follow up on the patient's condition after hospitalization.
 Conduct a pre-transfer assessment and stabilize the patient; order routine cardiac diagnostic tests per protocol; contact the receiving hospital emergency department to arrange transport; provide urgent care-level medical services until transport arrives; consult with the on-call cardiologist regarding initial management recommendations; and complete the transfer paperwork.
