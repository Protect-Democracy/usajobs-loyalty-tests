Specialized Experience: To qualify at the GS-15, you must possess the specialized experience, equivalent to the GS-14 level in the Federal service, or comparable experience not gained through federal service. For this position, specialized experience is defined as: Applying skills in managing portfolios, introducing new technical practices to operations where precedents do not exist. Assessing programs to determine where data and technology can be applied to make improvements in efficiencies and operations. Planning and implementing new operational programs; and providing leadership to large and complex organizations. In addition to the specialized experience described above, you must have related experience in each of the competencies listed below which must be supported by the information contained in your resume and other application materials: Leading, supervising, or managing information technology programs, projects, or portfolios that support mission-critical operations. Developing, implementing, or overseeing IT strategy, policies, governance processes, performance metrics, and long-range planning activities. Managing IT project portfolio activities, including demand management, project management, program/portfolio management, results management, prioritization, and resource allocation. Providing technical direction, oversight, or advisory support on IT initiatives involving classified, sensitive, intelligence, cybersecurity, data management, or enterprise IT environments. Translating customer or mission requirements into IT solutions, use cases, technical requirements, acquisition inputs, or program execution plans. Evaluating IT methods, systems, services, processes, or contractor performance to improve efficiency, effectiveness, compliance, cost effectiveness, or mission outcomes. Preparing and presenting briefings, reports, technical analyses, justifications, recommendations, or background papers to senior officials, stakeholders, or interagency partners. Coordinating with internal and external stakeholders, including senior leaders, mission users, contractors, other Federal agencies, or intelligence community partners, to resolve complex IT or operational issues. Exercising supervisory responsibilities such as assigning work, setting priorities, evaluating performance, identifying training needs, resolving employee issues, recommending selections or awards, and initiating corrective or disciplinary actions when appropriate.
